What this is

The fourth and final persona in the KYC set, and the one that looks like a footnote until you draw it. For investment purposes an OCI is treated on par with a non-resident Indian under FEMA: the same schemes, the same banking rails, the same repatriability rules. The divergence is documentary, since there is no Indian passport to present. But there is one place where the difference is substantive rather than clerical, and drawing the persona as a variant note on the NRI diagram would have buried it.

Reading the blue markers

Nodes and notes drawn in blue mark where this flow departs in substance from the non-resident path, rather than merely restating it. There are three, and they are the reason this is a diagram and not a paragraph.

Drawing standard

Fixed across the series. Five of these are checked when the diagram is built, and the file is not written if any check fails.

  • Orthogonal only. Every connector segment is exactly horizontal or vertical. No diagonals. Checked.
  • Turns at the midpoint. Where a connector needs two turns, the crossing run sits at the exact midpoint of the span, so both legs are equal.
  • Edge attachment, separated. Connectors leave and enter only on a shape's edge, never at a corner and never inside it. Where several arrows meet the same shape they attach at different points along that edge rather than stacking on the midpoint, so each stays traceable. On a decision diamond that means a point along the relevant slanted face.
  • No connector crosses a shape. Checked.
  • Every arrowhead lands on a shape outline. Never in empty space. This matters most on a decision diamond, whose bounding box is not its outline, so a point that looks like the top edge floats clear of the shape. Checked.
  • No two arrowheads coincide. Same-colour heads must also stand clear of each other. Checked.
  • Phase bands occupy clear strips. A band is placed only in a horizontal strip containing no shape at all, so a band label can never collide with a box. Placement is computed, not eyeballed. Checked.
  • Colour carries meaning. Purple is the main path, amber is remediation, red is a blocking failure, teal is a shortcut or handoff.
  • Room over compactness. Height is unconstrained, so type stays large and nothing is crowded.

The flow

Where this sits in the KYC set

Resident Individual · Minor · NRI · OCI. Four personas, one drawing standard, and a shared spine underneath: capture, pre-flight gates, verification modality, KRA status resolution, transactable identity. What differs by persona is which gates exist and what each one is testing.

The divergence that matters: citizenship and residence are two questions

A non-resident Indian is an Indian citizen, so the FATCA question is answered entirely by where they are tax resident. An OCI is a foreign citizen. If that citizenship is American, they are a US person for FATCA reporting regardless of where in the world they live. An OCI resident in Singapore holding a US passport still falls inside the US reporting regime, and a design that tests only country of residence will miss them completely.

So this flow carries two gates where the NRI flow carries one: a citizenship test for US person status, and a residence test for the fund-house acceptance constraint. The blocked-country check is also run twice, on residence and on citizenship, because for this persona the two need not be the same country.

Two smaller divergences that stall real files

Names do not agree, and it is nobody's error. Transliteration from an Indian name into a foreign passport produces spellings that do not match the PAN, and surnames appear, disappear or merge across passport, OCI card and PAN. The mismatch is real to a verifier even when the person is obviously the same, so it needs an affidavit or a correction rather than an override. This is the most common OCI-specific stall and it has no equivalent on the NRI path.

A card can be valid and still stale. The OCI card is tied to the passport it was issued against, so a renewed passport can leave a perfectly valid card out of date. That is not an obvious failure mode and it is worth a gate rather than a note.

And one case that runs the other way. An OCI who has been in India long enough to be a person resident under FEMA invests on a resident basis. The card in their hand says OCI and the status that governs says resident. It is the status that governs, and the flow routes them to the Resident Individual path rather than treating the card as decisive.

Grounded against, and three things to confirm

The Investor reference, section 13, for the OCI profile and its parity with the non-resident treatment, and sections 4 and 5 for the documentation backbone and the KYC machinery. J1 for the persona branch. The register at v0.17 for D2 on the Registered relaxation and the Validated-first build posture, D20 on US and Canada acceptance, D23 on the absence of cross-border UPI, D27 on repatriable and non-repatriable folio segregation, D32 on per-scheme non-resident eligibility, and D41 on the V1 investor set.

Three points I have drawn as gates without being able to ground the detail, and which should be confirmed before the Onboarding PRD hardens. First, the treatment of a legacy PIO card: the merger into the OCI scheme is well established, but whether an unconverted card is accepted at the KRA in practice is a question for Compliance rather than an assumption. Second, the OCI card re-issue trigger points: the rules were relaxed in 2020 and I have deliberately drawn the gate without the specific ages, because coding a threshold from memory is exactly the kind of error this series is meant to avoid. Third, whether Aadhaar is realistically obtainable by an OCI living abroad, which is the same question the NRI diagram raised and which bites harder here. If the answer is that it is not, then Registered stops being an exception on this path and becomes the base case, and how much is built around the first-fund-house constraint changes accordingly.
