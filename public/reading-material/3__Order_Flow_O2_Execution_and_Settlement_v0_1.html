What this is

The part the firm watches rather than controls. Once an order is routed, every state that follows is learned from a feed. The diagram is built around two facts that are counter-intuitive and that most order defects trace back to: the price is not set when the investor taps confirm, and a purchase is not final when units appear.

Why the states are named rather than inferred

Placed, RoutedToRail, Acknowledged, Allotted, Settled exist as explicit states precisely so the platform can answer "where is my money" correctly at any moment. Inferring them from timestamps is how that answer becomes wrong.

Drawing standard

Fixed across the series. Five of these are checked when the diagram is built, and the file is not written if any check fails.

  • Orthogonal only. Every connector segment is exactly horizontal or vertical. No diagonals. Checked.
  • Turns at the midpoint. Where a connector needs two turns, the crossing run sits at the exact midpoint of the span, so both legs are equal.
  • Edge attachment, separated. Connectors leave and enter only on a shape's edge, never at a corner and never inside it. Where several arrows meet the same shape they attach at different points along that edge rather than stacking on the midpoint, so each stays traceable. On a decision diamond that means a point along the relevant slanted face.
  • No connector crosses a shape. Checked.
  • Every arrowhead lands on a shape outline. Never in empty space. This matters most on a decision diamond, whose bounding box is not its outline, so a point that looks like the top edge floats clear of the shape. Checked.
  • No two arrowheads coincide. Same-colour heads must also stand clear of each other. Checked.
  • Phase bands occupy clear strips. A band is placed only in a horizontal strip containing no shape at all, so a band label can never collide with a box. Placement is computed, not eyeballed. Checked.
  • Colour carries meaning. Purple is the main path, amber is remediation, red is a blocking failure, teal is a shortcut or handoff.
  • Room over compactness. Height is unconstrained, so type stays large and nothing is crowded.

The flow

Where this sits in the Order set

O1 Placement and pre-flight · O2 Execution, pricing and settlement · O3 Failure, rejection and reversal · O4 Systematic plans · O5 Switch and STP. One order spine with typed gates, plus the three things that refuse to sit on it: a plan is not its instalments, a switch is two orders with a dependency, and Failed and Reversed are separated by the allotment line. Mandate registration and authentication are deliberately absent throughout: anything registered now to debit later belongs to the Mandate journey, and these diagrams show only the attachment contract.

The realisation rule, and the stale assumption it kills

For a purchase, the applicable NAV is the NAV of the day the funds are realised and available to the AMC, regardless of when the order was placed or how large it was. The click commits the intent; the money landing fixes the price. An older rule applying realisation pricing only to purchases above two lakh was retired in early 2021, so any legacy assumption that small purchases price on the time-stamp day is stale and will misprice. There is one uniform rule for every ticket size. The consequence the model has to encode is that the applicable NAV is not determinable at Placed, which is exactly why Placed and Acknowledged are separate states, and that the payment rail's speed is a pricing input rather than a convenience.

The dangerous window

Between provisional allotment and confirmed realisation, units appear to exist and the money is not yet certain. The recorded position is that a confirmed holding is shown only once payment is confirmed cleared, and a purchase is final only when allotment and payment agree. What is not settled is whether a provisional allotment is surfaced at all, as a clearly marked in-progress state, or simply not shown until settled. That is a Folio PRD call and the diagram marks it rather than choosing.

Grounded against, and what to confirm

The Order reference for the lifecycle, cut-off and realisation rules, settlement and attribution; the Rails reference for the separation between the transaction rail and the daily mailback reconciliation feed. The register for D5 on cut-offs as daily-fetched parameters, D6 on settlement timelines, D36 on showing units before clearance, D37 on freshness, and D1 on the rail choice. Both the exact cut-off clock times and the per-scheme settlement cycles are drawn as parameters, not values, because the reference flags each as an operational figure to confirm with the RTA and operations rather than hard-code, and the settlement cycles continue to track a cash market moving toward shorter settlement.
