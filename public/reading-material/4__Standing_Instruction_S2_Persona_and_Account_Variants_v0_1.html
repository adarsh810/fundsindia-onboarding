What this is

The registration spine assumes a resident individual with a bank account in their own name. This diagram draws what changes when that assumption does not hold. One rule sits above every branch: the debited account must belong to the investor, or to the party the regulator names as acting for them. Third-party pay-in is not permitted, which turns what looks like a question of convenience into a question of eligibility.

Drawing standard

Fixed across the series. Five of these are checked when the diagram is built, and the file is not written if any check fails.

  • Orthogonal only. Every connector segment is exactly horizontal or vertical. No diagonals. Checked.
  • Turns at the midpoint. Where a connector needs two turns, the crossing run sits at the exact midpoint of the span, so both legs are equal.
  • Edge attachment, separated. Connectors leave and enter only on a shape's edge, never at a corner and never inside it. Where several arrows meet the same shape they attach at different points along that edge rather than stacking on the midpoint, so each stays traceable. On a decision diamond that means a point along the relevant slanted face.
  • No connector crosses a shape. Checked.
  • Every arrowhead lands on a shape outline. Never in empty space. This matters most on a decision diamond, whose bounding box is not its outline, so a point that looks like the top edge floats clear of the shape. Checked.
  • No two arrowheads coincide. Same-colour heads must also stand clear of each other. Checked.
  • Phase bands occupy clear strips. A band is placed only in a horizontal strip containing no shape at all, so a band label can never collide with a box. Placement is computed, not eyeballed. Checked.
  • Colour carries meaning. Purple is the main path, amber is remediation, red is a blocking failure, teal is a shortcut or handoff.
  • Room over compactness. Height is unconstrained, so type stays large and nothing is crowded.

The flow

Where this sits in the Standing Instruction set

S1 Mandate registration, the core spine · S2 Persona and account variants · S3 The debit cycle · S4 Amendment, pause and termination · S5 Non-bank standing instructions. The block is named for standing instructions rather than mandates because only one of the three plan types has a mandate at all. A SIP is funded from a bank account and needs an authority over it. An SWP pays money outward and an STP moves value between schemes inside one fund house, so neither ever pulls from an account and neither has a mandate to register, amend or revoke. S5 exists to make that difference explicit rather than let it be assumed away.

Two variants where the mandate carries a hidden expiry

The minor folio. The mandate sits on the guardian's account, because the minor is the holder but not the payer. The guardian's authority ends on the minor's eighteenth birthday, so a SIP on a minor folio has a built-in end date that has nothing to do with the mandate's own validity window. At majority the folio freezes, the plan pauses, and the mandate must be replaced by one on the now-adult's own account. Registering a ten-year mandate for a seventeen-year-old is setting up a surprise.

The non-resident folio. NRE and FCNR fund a repatriable folio, NRO funds a non-repatriable one. Because repatriability is one of the four dimensions a folio matches on, the account behind the mandate decides which folio the instalments can land in. A mandate on an NRO account cannot feed a repatriable folio, and the place to discover that is setup rather than the first debit. The rail question then answers itself, since there is no cross-border UPI in V1 and an eNACH on the NRE or NRO account is the only path.

Grounded against, and what I could not confirm

The Investor reference for the persona profiles, the operating authority and the repatriability class; the Order reference for the folio's four-way match and for third-party pay-in; J5 for the registration spine this page rejoins. The register for D23, D41 and D42. One branch is drawn on reasoning rather than on a documented rule. The joint-folio case, where a jointly operated folio cannot be expressed on a rail that authenticates a single account holder, follows from how the rails work rather than from anything written down in the canon, and it should be confirmed with the rail before it is coded as a block. If it turns out a jointly operated folio can carry a mandate authenticated by one holder, that branch disappears and the gate is simply wrong.
