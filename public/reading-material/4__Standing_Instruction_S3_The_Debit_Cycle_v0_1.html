What this is

Everything that happens between a live mandate and a collected instalment, drawn from the mandate's side rather than the plan's. The gates before the debit are about whether it can be presented at all; the handling after it is about what a failure means. The distinction the whole page turns on is that a failure pauses a plan and never touches the mandate, which stays Active and may still be backing other plans on the same account.

Drawing standard

Fixed across the series. Five of these are checked when the diagram is built, and the file is not written if any check fails.

  • Orthogonal only. Every connector segment is exactly horizontal or vertical. No diagonals. Checked.
  • Turns at the midpoint. Where a connector needs two turns, the crossing run sits at the exact midpoint of the span, so both legs are equal.
  • Edge attachment, separated. Connectors leave and enter only on a shape's edge, never at a corner and never inside it. Where several arrows meet the same shape they attach at different points along that edge rather than stacking on the midpoint, so each stays traceable. On a decision diamond that means a point along the relevant slanted face.
  • No connector crosses a shape. Checked.
  • Every arrowhead lands on a shape outline. Never in empty space. This matters most on a decision diamond, whose bounding box is not its outline, so a point that looks like the top edge floats clear of the shape. Checked.
  • No two arrowheads coincide. Same-colour heads must also stand clear of each other. Checked.
  • Phase bands occupy clear strips. A band is placed only in a horizontal strip containing no shape at all, so a band label can never collide with a box. Placement is computed, not eyeballed. Checked.
  • Colour carries meaning. Purple is the main path, amber is remediation, red is a blocking failure, teal is a shortcut or handoff.
  • Room over compactness. Height is unconstrained, so type stays large and nothing is crowded.

The flow

Where this sits in the Standing Instruction set

S1 Mandate registration, the core spine · S2 Persona and account variants · S3 The debit cycle · S4 Amendment, pause and termination · S5 Non-bank standing instructions. The block is named for standing instructions rather than mandates because only one of the three plan types has a mandate at all. A SIP is funded from a bank account and needs an authority over it. An SWP pays money outward and an STP moves value between schemes inside one fund house, so neither ever pulls from an account and neither has a mandate to register, amend or revoke. S5 exists to make that difference explicit rather than let it be assumed away.

No re-presentation, and what that costs

The eNACH and NACH rails support presenting a failed debit again, and the decision taken is not to use it. One presentation, one outcome. The reasoning on the credit side is real: strike counting becomes unambiguous, because one instalment produces exactly one outcome and three consecutive failures means three months rather than something that depends on attempt counts; the outcome is known on the debit date, so the investor can be told immediately; and there is one notice per date rather than a second-notice question.

The cost is equally real and should not be quietly absorbed. An instalment lost to a one-day balance shortfall is simply lost, and the money does not go in that month. The failure message is therefore the place to offer a manual purchase, because without that a strict no-retry policy reads as the platform silently skipping a month. There is also a rail-side condition on the decision holding at all: if Fintech Primitives or the sponsor bank re-presents by default, our position becomes a suppression requirement rather than an abstention, and that is an open ask.

Grounded against, and what is open

The Order reference, section 6, for the recurring-debit framework, the pre-debit notification and the mandate machinery. The register for D4, D9 and its no-re-presentation follow-on, D43, D44 and D46. Three things on this page are outstanding, and none of them is an open decision in the register sense. The multi-plan ceiling question is a follow-on to D44, which is closed: one mandate can back several plans within its limit, and what remains is whether a new plan that would breach the ceiling on a shared date is blocked at setup or allowed to fail on the day. The leaning is block-at-setup, since allow-and-fail knowingly feeds the D9 failure counter with a failure the investor was set up for. The per-bank caps are a follow-on to D31 and sit behind the ceiling gate. And the rail's default re-presentation behaviour is unknown, which the no-re-presentation position depends on. All three want closing in the same engineering session, since all three shape the same machinery.
