Standing Instruction S5: Non-Bank Standing Instructions

SWP and STP have no mandate, no ceiling, no validity window and no bank that can decline. What they have is a registered schedule and a dependency on units.

What this is, and why it is separate

A mandate is an authority over a bank account. An SWP pays money outward and an STP moves value between two schemes inside one fund house, so neither ever pulls from an account. There is nothing to authenticate on a recurring-debit rail, no ceiling, no validity window, and no bank that can decline. This page exists so that difference is drawn rather than assumed, because the instinct carried over from SIP setup is to expect machinery that is not there.

What is deliberately not repeated here

Plan states, pause, resume and completion belong to the plan lifecycle diagram, and the two-leg mechanics of an STP cycle belong to the switch and STP diagram. This page covers what is registered, what it needs, and what happens on a scheduled date when the units are short.

Drawing standard

Fixed across the series. Five of these are checked when the diagram is built, and the file is not written if any check fails.

  • Orthogonal only. Every connector segment is exactly horizontal or vertical. No diagonals. Checked.
  • Turns at the midpoint. Where a connector needs two turns, the crossing run sits at the exact midpoint of the span, so both legs are equal.
  • Edge attachment, separated. Connectors leave and enter only on a shape's edge, never at a corner and never inside it. Where several arrows meet the same shape they attach at different points along that edge rather than stacking on the midpoint, so each stays traceable. On a decision diamond that means a point along the relevant slanted face.
  • No connector crosses a shape. Checked.
  • Every arrowhead lands on a shape outline. Never in empty space. This matters most on a decision diamond, whose bounding box is not its outline, so a point that looks like the top edge floats clear of the shape. Checked.
  • No two arrowheads coincide. Same-colour heads must also stand clear of each other. Checked.
  • Phase bands occupy clear strips. A band is placed only in a horizontal strip containing no shape at all, so a band label can never collide with a box. Placement is computed, not eyeballed. Checked.
  • Colour carries meaning. Purple is the main path, amber is remediation, red is a blocking failure, teal is a shortcut or handoff.
  • Room over compactness. Height is unconstrained, so type stays large and nothing is crowded.

The flow

Where this sits in the Standing Instruction set

S1 Mandate registration, the core spine · S2 Persona and account variants · S3 The debit cycle · S4 Amendment, pause and termination · S5 Non-bank standing instructions. The block is named for standing instructions rather than mandates because only one of the three plan types has a mandate at all. A SIP is funded from a bank account and needs an authority over it. An SWP pays money outward and an STP moves value between schemes inside one fund house, so neither ever pulls from an account and neither has a mandate to register, amend or revoke. S5 exists to make that difference explicit rather than let it be assumed away.

There is a registration, just not a bank authority

Worth stating plainly because the absence of a mandate invites the wrong conclusion. The schedule is recorded with the fund house through the transaction rail, not held privately by us, and it can be rejected: an unsupported frequency for that scheme, a source and target combination the fund house does not permit, an amount below a scheme minimum, or a scheme closed to the transaction type. What is absent is the bank authority, not the registration. The corollary is that amendment is much lighter than on a mandate, because no authority is being widened: amount, frequency and end date can change, and the schedule can be paused or cancelled, with no re-authentication anywhere.

A short holding is not a failure, and the exhaustion trap

When the units are not there on a scheduled date, the instalment simply does not generate. No money moved, nothing was presented, no bank declined anything, so there is nothing to fail. It is recorded as information and counts toward no tally: the three-strike pause belongs to payment failures on a SIP and has no meaning here. Treating a short holding as a failure would pause a plan behaving exactly as designed.

The related trap sits on the SWP side. A withdrawal schedule that outruns the holding will exhaust the folio, and the instruction then keeps generating nothing every month rather than ending. An end condition, or a warning as the holding falls, is the difference between a plan that completes and one that quietly does nothing.

Grounded against

The Order reference, section 5, for the SWP and STP funding shapes, for the fact that neither needs a payment mandate, for the one-time funding of an STP source scheme being a separate purchase rather than part of the transfer, and for a short holding being recorded as information rather than as a failure. The Instrument reference for the scheme-level minimums and permitted transaction types the registration gate reads. This page introduces no new state and opens no new decision; its purpose is to draw a boundary that already exists in the canon but that a mandate-shaped mental model tends to erase.
