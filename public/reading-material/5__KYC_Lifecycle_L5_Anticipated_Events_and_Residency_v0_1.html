What this is

Two halves. The first is the set of events that change a value or add a check without rerouting the record: periodic re-KYC, contact and bank changes, dormancy, accreditation. They share one property worth drawing, which is that each carries a verification step that can itself fail, and a failed verification is a downgrade. The second half is residency change, which is on this page only because it has not been ruled on, and which is drawn as a blast radius rather than as a design.

Drawing standard

Fixed across the series. Five of these are checked when the diagram is built, and the file is not written if any check fails.

  • Orthogonal only. Every connector segment is exactly horizontal or vertical. No diagonals. Checked.
  • Turns at the midpoint. Where a connector needs two turns, the crossing run sits at the exact midpoint of the span, so both legs are equal.
  • Edge attachment, separated. Connectors leave and enter only on a shape's edge, never at a corner and never inside it. Where several arrows meet the same shape they attach at different points along that edge rather than stacking on the midpoint, so each stays traceable. On a decision diamond that means a point along the relevant slanted face.
  • No connector crosses a shape. Checked.
  • Every arrowhead lands on a shape outline. Never in empty space. This matters most on a decision diamond, whose bounding box is not its outline, so a point that looks like the top edge floats clear of the shape. Checked.
  • No two arrowheads coincide. Same-colour heads must also stand clear of each other. Checked.
  • Phase bands occupy clear strips. A band is placed only in a horizontal strip containing no shape at all, so a band label can never collide with a box. Placement is computed, not eyeballed. Checked.
  • Colour carries meaning. Purple is the main path, amber is remediation, red is a blocking failure, teal is a shortcut or handoff.
  • Room over compactness. Height is unconstrained, so type stays large and nothing is crowded.

The flow

J1 drew the front door; this set owns everything that happens to a transactable identity afterwards. It invents no status and no transition: the ladder and the transition table belong to the Investor reference, the registries and their failure modes belong to the Rails reference, and the pre-flight gate that consumes the status belongs to the Order reference.

The routine event that downgrades a record

An investor updating a mobile number is doing housekeeping. Mobile, email and bank account are validated attributes, so the change triggers a re-validation, and if the new number cannot be verified the record moves. The investor is then blocked by an action they took to keep things tidy. The message has to connect the two explicitly, or the downgrade looks arbitrary and the investor has no way to work out what they did.

Residency change: five things move, in four different places

The KYC record moves, because residency is an attribute the registry holds. The bank account class changes, from resident to NRE or NRO or back. The folio's repatriability tag is re-cut, and repatriable and non-repatriable holdings must sit on separate folios. Tax residency and the withholding regime change, altering what is deducted at every redemption. And FATCA and CRS reporting is pulled in or dropped depending on direction.

The homecoming leg is the one that gets dropped. Teams build the departure, because the investor announces it and there is a natural prompt. The return leg has no prompt: an investor who moves back rarely thinks to tell their distributor, so the record silently misstates their status and keeps doing so. The reference flags this as routinely under-built and it is worth naming explicitly in whatever ruling is made.

The open decision, and what is owed regardless

D47 is still open with Product and Compliance: whether the residency-change servicing flow is in V1 scope, and whether it is its own journey. The recommendation on record is to keep the trigger bulleted, defer the full flow to a dedicated journey, and rule separately on V1 scope. This page deliberately draws the blast radius rather than a design, so that it is useful for making the decision rather than presupposing it.

One thing I would argue is owed even at minimum scope, and it is a position rather than a documented requirement: the platform must detect a mismatch when one surfaces, refuse to transact on a record it knows is wrong, and route the investor to a human. Doing nothing is not the low-scope option. It is the option where the record quietly lies about the investor's tax position and the platform keeps transacting on it.
