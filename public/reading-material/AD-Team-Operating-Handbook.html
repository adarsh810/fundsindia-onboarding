Operating handbook

How we work here

This is the operating manual for the team. The framework doc, Change, Consistency and Propagation, describes how the system works. This describes what you do inside it: which tool for which job, what is expected of your artifacts, how to work well with Claude, and how we keep the whole thing honest.

It is kept deliberately thin. Where a mechanic lives in the framework doc or a living artifact, this points there rather than repeating it, because a handbook that duplicates is a handbook that rots.

1Rule zero

Two rules sit above everything else. If you are ever unsure whether something is allowed, these decide it, and when they are unclear, you stop and ask the steward rather than guess.

No real customer data leaves production

Regulated customer data (KYC, transaction and financial PII) lives only in the India production estate. It never appears in a PRD, a design doc, a ticket, a test fixture, a prompt, or any non-prod environment, not even as a realistic-looking sample. Everywhere else uses synthetic or de-identified data.

This is what keeps the whole toolchain compliant. The tools that process outside India stay out of scope only because they never hold regulated data.

Claude proposes, humans dispose

No Claude tool, in any surface, deploys to production, applies a migration to production, or touches real customer data. Claude drafts; a human approves through the normal flow. Approval for the gated actions sits with senior engineers and the lead.

Mechanics and the residency rationale are in the framework doc, sections 6 and 7.

2Which tool for which job

Most thrash on a tool-rich team comes from work landing in the wrong place, where it is never captured. The rule of thumb: if the output should outlive the conversation, it belongs in a tool that writes to the source of truth, not in a chat window.

Claude ChatThinking out loud, drafting prose, research, a quick question, rubber-ducking a design before it is written down.Anything that must land in the repo or a canonical doc. Do that in Code or Cowork so it is captured.

3Your role

Everyone owns specific artifacts and is accountable for keeping their typed links current. Beyond that, here is what is expected by role.

Product managers

You own Strategy, PRDs, entities and journeys, in Confluence. Your artifact is done when it names the entities and journeys it touches with typed links, every open question is filed in the Open Decisions Register rather than left floating in prose, success metrics are stated, and it is marked status: locked. When a question needs Business, Compliance or Legal, you raise an open decision rather than assuming the answer.

Designers

You own UI/UX journeys and design, in Figma, linked from the relevant journey and design docs. Your work is done when the design source is linked (not pasted) and the journey it serves is referenced, so a change in either side surfaces as a signal rather than a silent divergence.

Engineers and architects

You own technical design docs and code, in the repo. A design doc is done when it carries its front-matter (what it realises, depends on, and any decision it raises), records the alternatives considered, and notes data-model impact. Code is done when it has tests carrying the right @covers links and passes the gates. When you hit a constraint that changes scope, you raise an open decision up to the PM or designer; you do not quietly absorb it or quietly drop a requirement.

Business, Compliance, Legal

You answer the open decisions routed to you. Each carries an owner and a due date; an unanswered decision is a blocker on someone else's work, so the expectation is a clear yes, no, or "here is the constraint," not silence.

Framework steward

You own the integrity tooling, the Drift Register, the always-on Cowork machine, and triage. You read the nightly consistency output, route drift to the right owners, and keep the source of truth honest over time. This is the role whose absence quietly sinks a documentation system, so it is named and owned, not assumed.

4Artifacts: conventions and templates

Consistency is produced at the source by these conventions, not enforced after the fact. Every artifact carries front-matter with typed links so the dependency map stays navigable both ways. status is draft until reviewed and then locked; the drift checks run against locked artifacts, and changing a locked one updates its version stamp and flows through normal review.

The front-matter (what it realises, depends on, and raises) is in the framework doc, section 2. The body carries: context, the decision, alternatives considered, data-model impact, risks, test plan, and links to the PRD and journey it serves.

5Working with Claude

Claude is a capable teammate when pointed at the right context and reviewed like any other contributor. A few norms make the difference between leverage and mess.

  • Point at canonical context, not fragments. Give Claude the relevant PRD, design doc and the dependency map, rather than pasting snippets. It reasons better over the whole tree than over an excerpt.
  • Keep agent PRs small and single-purpose. They carry their front-matter and include tests with the right @covers links, the same as a human PR. A sprawling agent PR is hard to review and is usually doing too much.
  • You are the author of record. A PR you open with Claude's help is yours to stand behind. Review AI-drafted work as critically as you would a colleague's, more so on anything subtle.
  • Brief like a spec. State the goal, the constraints, and what "done" looks like. For anything large, ask for the plan or the diff before the change.
  • If a task seems to need prod or real data, stop. That is the signal to route it to the steward, not to find a workaround. Rule zero does not bend under deadline.

The consistency loop that Claude runs over the docs is described in the framework doc, section 5.

6Cadences

The operating model has a light heartbeat, kept minimal on purpose, because the framework exists to support delivery and not the other way around.

  • Nightly. The Cowork consistency run produces Drift Register entries. The steward triages them the next morning and routes each to its owner.
  • Weekly. A short open-decisions review to clear blockers and confirm every open decision has an owner and a due date, plus a glance at drift burndown so inconsistencies do not pile up.
  • Per PR. Small, single-purpose, gates green. Drift warnings are judged, not ignored; a warning that is fine gets a one-line note, a warning that matters becomes a fix.

7Your first week

A concrete path in rather than a cold start.

  1. Read the framework doc and this handbook end to end. They are short by design.
  2. Get access: Confluence, GitHub, and the Claude suite. Install Claude Code and Claude Cowork.
  3. With a buddy, read the canonical artifacts for your area and learn the front-matter convention by tracing a few existing links.
  4. Make one real change end to end: a small PR or a PRD edit, taken all the way through the flow with your buddy reviewing. Doing it once beats reading about it five times.

If you are a PM moving into the repo, the expectation is comfort over roughly three months, not on day one. You start by reading design docs through the Confluence mirror and contributing via comments, and graduate into repo authorship over the quarter.

8Glossary

  • Canonical home. The one place an artifact officially lives. Every artifact has exactly one.
  • The mirror. The read-only reflection of repo design docs in Confluence. Generated, never hand-edited.
  • Typed link. A dependency that records its nature (realises, depends on, raises) and is navigable both ways.
  • Version stamp. The Confluence version an artifact was last reconciled against; a higher current version is the drift signal.
  • Open Decisions Register. The list of open questions routed to Business, Compliance, Legal, Product or Tech, each owned and dated.
  • Drift Register. The machine-maintained list of inconsistencies among locked artifacts.
  • Locked. An artifact reviewed and treated as a source of truth; drift checks run against it.
  • The firewall. The rule that keeps real regulated data inside production and out of everything else.
  • Steward. The owner of the integrity tooling, the Drift Register, and triage.

This handbook is itself an artifact in the system: its canonical home is Confluence, the steward owns it, and it is subject to the same drift checks as everything else. If it cannot live inside the model it describes, the model has a hole.
