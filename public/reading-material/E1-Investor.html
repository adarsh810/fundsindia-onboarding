The Investor Entity

A master reference across every eligible persona and every facet that shapes how an investor behaves in an Indian wealth platform.

Statusv0.3: NRI relaxation grounded (D2)
DateJune 2026
AudienceEngineering & Product
TypeMaster reference (not a PRD)

What this document is

A single, exhaustive reference view of the Investor entity across all of its facets. It catalogues every eligible persona and describes, at a consistent depth, what each persona may invest in, how it is verified, how it is taxed, who may operate it, how it holds and pays, how it passes on, what it reports, and how it changes state over time. It is meant to be the place a product manager or engineer comes to understand who an investor is before any specific flow is designed.

What it is not

It is not a PRD and not a build plan. It carries no phasing, sequencing, or platform-architecture decisions. Individual PRDs will later draw on subsets of this material for specific flows. It is also not legal, tax, or compliance advice; the regulatory and tax figures are current to the date above and several of them move with each Union Budget and SEBI circular.

Scope

Personas in scope: all natural-person personas (Resident Individual, Minor, Non-Resident Indian, Overseas Citizen of India, Foreign National resident in India) and all non-individual entity personas commonly seen in retail and affluent wealth (HUF, Sole Proprietorship, Partnership Firm, LLP, Company, Private/Family Trust, Public/Charitable Trust, Society/AOP/BOI). Institutional and specialised personas (FPI, banks, insurers, pension and provident funds, government bodies) are deliberately excluded.

Instruments in scope: Mutual Funds, Specialised Investment Funds (SIF), Bonds, Fixed Deposits, and Equities. Digital gold, NPS, and insurance are out of scope for this reference.

Joint holding is covered as a cross-cutting holding mode rather than a persona, because in law it is a configuration layered on top of natural-person personas, not a distinct investor identity. The reasoning is set out in Part IV.

Conventions used in this document

The document is built on a common spine plus per-persona overlay structure. Facets that behave identically across most personas (the KYC machinery, the tax engine, the documentation backbone) are described once, in depth, in Part II. Each persona profile in Part III then states only what that persona changes about the spine. This keeps the depth consistent and prevents the same paragraph being copied thirteen times.

Three callout treatments recur:

Special attention

Open: verify before relying

Part I
The Investor entity model

1.What an investor persona is in this domain

A persona is not a label attached to an account. It is a stateful entity carrying a set of attributes, from which most of its observable behaviour can be derived, and which moves between states over its life.

It is tempting to treat eligibility, KYC, and tax as three independent axes that vary by persona. They are not independent. They are largely downstream of a smaller set of attributes the entity carries:

  • Residential and FEMA status (resident, non-resident, person of Indian origin), which drives eligibility limits, account type, and repatriation.
  • Tax residency and home jurisdiction, which drives withholding, treaty relief, and foreign reporting consequences such as US PFIC exposure.
  • Legal nature (natural person, undivided family, body corporate, trust, unincorporated association), which drives who may act, what documents establish the entity, and how it dissolves or devolves.
  • Operating-authority model (self, guardian, Karta, authorised signatory with resolution, power-of-attorney holder), which drives transaction authorisation.
  • Holding-mode capability (single or joint, statement-of-account or demat, repatriable or non-repatriable), which colours the entire holding.
  • Lifecycle state (for example a minor before majority, a record before KYC validation, a holding in transmission), which gates what is permitted right now.

Key principle

Eligibility, KYC, and tax are best modelled as functions of an investor's attributes and current lifecycle state, not as free-standing properties to be set per persona. The attributes are the source of truth; the three familiar axes are projections of them. The part a pure lookup table misses is the lifecycle: the same investor produces different correct answers before and after a residency change, a coming-of-age, or a death.

2.The facet taxonomy

Each persona is examined through the same set of facets, in the same order, so the profiles in Part III can be read side by side. The facets are:

  1. Definition and defining attributes: who this is, and the attribute values that distinguish it.
  2. Instrument eligibility: what it may hold across MF, SIF, Bonds, FD, Equities, and on what condition.
  3. KYC: the documents, the permitted verification modalities, and any status constraints.
  4. Tax overlay: what it changes about the instrument-driven tax base.
  5. Operating authority: who is permitted to act on the account.
  6. Holding mode: single or joint, SOA or demat, repatriability.
  7. Banking and payment: the accounts and rails it can use.
  8. Nomination and transmission: how the holding passes on.
  9. Reporting and attribution: the regulatory reporting it triggers.
  10. Special attention and opens: the unusual and the unverified.

Part II
The common spine

Nine facets that behave the same way across most personas are described here once, in depth. Every persona profile in Part III refers back to this spine and states only its deviations.

4.Identifiers and the documentation backbone

Every investor record, individual or entity, rests on the same backbone of identifiers and supporting documents. The persona changes which documents establish identity, not the existence of the backbone.

PAN is the anchor.

A Permanent Account Number is mandatory for every securities-market investment in scope, with only very narrow exceptions. For individual personas the PAN must also be linked with Aadhaar; an unlinked PAN is treated as inoperative and drops the KYC record to a blocked state, which stops even redemptions. PAN-Aadhaar linkage is therefore a pre-flight check, not a post-submission failure.

The Officially Valid Document (OVD).

Identity and address are established by an OVD: passport, voter ID, driving licence, Aadhaar, NREGA job card, or a National Population Register letter. A 2024 tightening removed utility bills and bank statements as acceptable standalone OVDs for primary KYC, which is worth designing around because older records relied on them.

FATCA / CRS self-certification.

A self-certification of tax residency is universal, for individuals and entities alike. It is the hook on which foreign-reporting and the US/Canada restrictions hang.

Ultimate Beneficial Owner (UBO) identification.

For every non-individual persona, identity verification does not stop at the entity. Under the PMLA framework the controlling natural persons must be traced and verified: conventionally those holding more than 25 percent for companies, and more than 15 percent for partnerships, trusts, and unincorporated bodies, or control exercised by other means. Layered ownership (an entity owned by an entity owned by a trust) makes this a graph rather than a list.

Supporting items.

A recent photograph, a bank-account proof for the pay-in and pay-out account, and a signature or e-sign complete the backbone for individuals. Entities add their constitutive documents, an authorising resolution, and the authorised-signatory set.

5.The KYC machinery

KYC in India is three things stacked: the rail the record lives on, the method used to verify, and the status that the method earns. All three are described here; persona-specific deviations are in Part III.

The three rails

Securities-market KYC for MF, SIF, Bonds, and Equities runs through the SEBI-registered KYC Registration Agencies (KRAs) (CVL, NDML/NSE, CAMS, KFin) and feeds the Central KYC Registry (CKYC) maintained by CERSAI, which issues a fourteen-digit identifier and spans banking, insurance, and securities. Fixed deposits sit on the banking KYC regime under the RBI, not the KRA securities framework, though CKYC is the shared backbone.

Special attention

A "validated" securities KYC does not automatically clear a fixed deposit. The FD partner bank runs its own KYC under the RBI regime. The same captured documents can be reused, but FD onboarding is a separate satisfaction, not a free ride on the KRA status. Treat FD as a distinct KYC surface even when the investor is already fully KYC'd for mutual funds.

The status taxonomy (revised through 2024)

Effective 1 April 2024, SEBI directed the KRAs to bucket every record into three functional states, with a fourth for rejection.1

Registered (also shown as Verified)Continued investment with existing AMCs; revalidation needed for a new fund house.Documents could not be database-verified, but mobile and email are confirmed.

The distinction between Validated and Registered is the engine of the whole regime: Validated means the record reconciled against government databases, Registered means it did not but is good enough to keep transacting where the relationship already exists.

Special attention

The ₹50,000-per-AMC ceiling on OTP-only eKYC is the single most consequential modality fact for an affluent book. An affluent investor breaches it almost immediately, so OTP-only is unsuitable as the sole onboarding path for anyone with meaningful ticket sizes. A full-KYC modality (video or biometric) must be used at onboarding for that segment. The ceiling lifts only because the in-person verification is treated as completed, which in turn depends on the agency being a recognised KUA or sub-KUA, not merely on the technology working.

Periodic re-KYC

KYC is not a one-time event. Re-verification is risk-based and time-bound: broadly every two years for high-risk clients, eight for medium, and ten for low-risk under the banking regime, with the KRA framework layering its own re-validation triggers (the push from Registered to Validated being the live example). A re-KYC scheduler keyed to a risk classification belongs in the model, not a single onboarding capture.

6.The tax engine

In India, tax is overwhelmingly a function of instrument category, holding period, and acquisition date, not persona. Persona applies a smaller set of overlays on top. This section is the base; the overlays are in each persona profile.

Three pivot dates govern everything below: 1 April 2023 (debt funds lose long-term benefit), 23 July 2024 (the capital-gains overhaul), and 1 April 2025 (the Specified Mutual Fund definition narrows).

Capital-gains base, transfers on or after 23 July 2024

Resident-individual base. Persona overlays modify withholding and certain exemptions, rarely the headline rate.
InstrumentShort-termLong-termLT line

Specified Mutual Funds (debt)SlabNo LT benefit: always slab (Sec 50AA)Always ST
Residual MF bucket (gold, international, many FoFs, 35–65% hybrids)Slab12.5% without indexation24 months

Bond / G-Sec coupon & FD interestTaxed as income at slab; not capital gains.n/a

The equity regime moved on 23 July 2024: long-term equity rose to 12.5 percent on gains above a raised ₹1.25 lakh exemption, and short-term on STT-paid listed equity and equity funds rose to 20 percent.3 Indexation was largely removed across asset classes, with a single exception letting resident individuals and HUFs choose 12.5 percent without indexation or 20 percent with indexation on immovable property acquired before that date.

The mutual fund buckets

This is where the real complexity sits, and where the instrument, not the persona, decides the answer.

  • Equity-oriented fund. A scheme with at least 65 percent in domestic listed equity. Taxed as equity above. A fund-of-funds qualifies as equity-oriented only if at least 90 percent is in units of a fund that itself holds at least 90 percent domestic listed equity.
  • Specified Mutual Fund. Under Section 50AA, units acquired on or after 1 April 2023 are deemed short-term regardless of holding period, so always at slab with no long-term benefit.4 The definition narrowed from 1 April 2025 to mean a fund investing more than 65 percent in debt and money-market instruments.
  • Residual bucket. Funds that are neither equity-oriented nor Specified, including gold funds, international funds, and hybrids between 35 and 65 percent equity. From 1 April 2025 these get 24-month, 12.5-percent-without-indexation long-term treatment, and slab for short-term. The 2025 narrowing is precisely what pulled gold and international funds out of the punitive debt bucket they had been swept into in 2023.

Open: verify with Tax counsel

Some early reporting placed the effective date for parts of the gold/international reclassification at April 2026 rather than April 2025. If a specific gold-FoF or international-fund treatment becomes load-bearing for a customer, confirm the exact effective date and the fund's current portfolio classification rather than relying on the clean "April 2025" version.

Dividends and surcharge

Dividends are taxable in the investor's hands at slab (the dividend distribution tax regime was abolished in 2020), with TDS on the way out. A useful structural nuance for high earners: the surcharge on capital gains under Sections 111A and 112A is capped at 15 percent, so the enhanced 25 and 37 percent surcharge bands do not reach those gains. A 4 percent health-and-education cess sits on top of tax-plus-surcharge throughout.

7.Operating authority and mandates

Who may act on an account is a first-class attribute, because the transaction-authorisation path depends on it. The models in scope:

ModelWho actsApplies to
SelfThe investor.Resident Individual, NRI, OCI, Foreign National.
GuardianNatural or court-appointed guardian, until the minor's majority.Minor.
KartaThe Karta of the family.HUF.
Authorised signatory + resolutionNamed individuals, empowered by a board or partners' resolution that also defines limits and signing rules.Company, LLP, Partnership, Trust, Society/AOP/BOI.

Joint operation has its own modes that change whose authorisation a transaction needs: Jointly (all must sign), Either or Survivor and Anyone or Survivor (any one may act, survivorship on death), and Former or Survivor (the first holder acts while alive). The operation mode is therefore not cosmetic; it is an input to the authorisation engine.

8.Holding and ownership modes

Three independent dimensions describe how a holding is owned. They combine, and they are attributes of the holding, not of the persona alone.

Single or joint.

A holding may be in one name or held jointly by two or more natural persons. Joint is covered in full in Part IV; here it is enough to note that the operation mode chosen at the joint level feeds the authorisation engine in Section 7.

Statement-of-account (SOA) or demat.

Mutual fund units can be held either in SOA form (the registrar's records, no demat needed) or dematerialised in a demat account. Equities require demat. Bonds may be held in demat or, for some government securities, in dedicated gilt accounts. The holding form has downstream consequences for nomination, transmission, and pledging, so it is a modelled attribute rather than an implementation detail.

Repatriable or non-repatriable.

Key principle

For NRI and OCI holdings, repatriability is not a tax detail; it colours the entire holding. A holding funded from an NRE or FCNR account is repatriable: proceeds can be sent abroad. A holding funded from an NRO account is non-repatriable, with only limited annual remittance permitted. Repatriability follows the money in, dictates where redemption proceeds can be routed, and belongs at the holding level in the model, not as a profile-level flag.

9.Banking and payment rails

The accounts and rails an investor can use vary sharply by persona, and this is the facet most likely to surprise a team that has built for residents only.

Resident Individual, HUFAny resident savings / current accountUPI, NACH and eNACH, netbanking, IMPS/RTGS/NEFT

Special attention

NRI and OCI banking is the trap. The account type is not a preference; it determines repatriability of everything bought through it, and ordinary domestic UPI from a foreign mobile number is generally not available, though cross-border UPI rails are expanding. A flow designed around UPI and a single resident savings account will not extend to a non-resident without rework.

One regulatory constraint sits above all the rails and applies regardless of persona: the SEBI rule against pooling of investor funds and units by mutual fund distributors and platforms. Money and units must flow on a non-pooled basis. This is a fact about the rails, recorded here because it shapes any payment design that touches these personas.

10.Nomination, succession and transmission

How a holding passes on differs by persona and by holding mode, and the event is a first-class lifecycle transition, not an edge case.

Nomination.

For mutual fund folios and demat accounts, nomination is effectively mandatory with an explicit opt-out: the investor must either nominate or consciously decline. The nomination is the instruction that drives transmission on death.

Transmission, by holding type.

  • Single holding with nominee: units or securities transmit to the nominee on production of death proof, the nominee then completing their own KYC.
  • Joint holding: survivorship governs. On the death of one holder, the holding vests in the survivor or survivors per the operation mode, ahead of any nominee.
  • HUF: on the Karta's death the role devolves to the next senior coparcener; the family continues as the entity rather than the holding being distributed.
  • Trust, company, partnership, LLP, society: the entity persists through the death of any individual; transmission instead follows the entity's own succession of trustees, directors, or partners, and ultimately its dissolution.

Special attention

Residents face no TDS on mutual fund redemptions; they self-assess through advance tax. For NRIs and OCIs this inverts entirely: tax is withheld at source on each redemption, and a certificate must be issued. The same redemption event therefore produces a fundamentally different obligation depending on a single attribute, residential status. This is not a footnote to a payout flow; it is a fork in it.

12.The home-jurisdiction overlay

For the cross-border personas, India is not the only taxing and reporting authority. The investor's country of residence is an attribute that changes both what they can hold and how it is taxed and reported.

  • DTAA. India's Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements can reduce Indian withholding on interest, dividends, and gains. Claiming the benefit requires a Tax Residency Certificate from the home country and a Form 10F filed with the Indian payor.5
  • US PFIC. For US persons, Indian mutual funds are typically Passive Foreign Investment Companies, whose punitive US tax treatment can make Indian MF investing actively unattractive on the home side, independent of Indian rules.
  • CRS jurisdiction. The country of tax residence determines where the investor's holdings are reported under the Common Reporting Standard.

Open: verify with Compliance and Tax counsel

US- and Canada-resident NRIs and OCIs are restricted or refused by several AMCs because of FATCA reporting burden, and the US PFIC overlay compounds it. The exact set of AMCs that accept US/Canada persons, and the operational handling, should be confirmed with Compliance and the relevant AMCs rather than assumed. Country of residence must be a modelled field, not onboarding free text.

Part III
Persona profiles

Each profile states only what the persona changes about the common spine. Facets appear in the same order throughout so the profiles can be read side by side.

13.Natural persons

Resident Individual

Definition and attributesAn individual resident in India under FEMA and the Income Tax Act, transacting on their own PAN. This is the baseline persona; every other profile is described as a deviation from it.
Instrument eligibilityEligible for all five: MF, SIF (subject to the ₹10 lakh minimum), Bonds, FD, and Equities. No persona-level eligibility gate.
KYCThe full modality menu is available. Because the OTP-only ceiling of ₹50,000 per AMC per year is breached almost immediately at affluent ticket sizes, a full-KYC modality (video or biometric) must be used at onboarding for this segment.
Tax overlayThe base in Section 6 applies as written. Two privileges are persona-restricted to resident individuals (and HUFs): adjusting long-term gains against any unused basic exemption limit (with the specific exception that Section 112A equity LTCG cannot be so adjusted), and the property indexation option for property bought before 23 July 2024. Crucially, residents face no TDS on MF redemptions and self-assess via advance tax.
Operating authoritySelf.
Holding modeSingle or joint; SOA or demat; repatriability not applicable.
Banking and paymentAny resident account; the full rail set including UPI.
Nomination and transmissionNominate or explicit opt-out; transmission to nominee, or survivorship if joint.
ReportingFATCA/CRS self-certification; EUIN/ARN attribution; SFT above thresholds. No at-source TDS on MF.
Special attention and opensNone unusual. This is the reference case.

Minor

Definition and attributesAn individual below eighteen. The holding is in the minor's name but the minor is not the acting party and, for income-tax purposes during minority, not even the tax subject.
Instrument eligibilityMF, Bonds, and FD are available through the guardian. SIF is eligible in principle but unusual given the ₹10 lakh minimum and the risk profile. Equities are the exception: a minor may hold a demat operated by the guardian and may receive shares (transmission, gifts, IPO allotment) but may not actively trade the secondary market, and intraday and derivatives are barred. The rule of thumb is hold yes, trade no.
KYCThe minor is not the KYC subject. The guardian must hold full, valid KYC. Submitted documents are the minor's date-of-birth proof, the guardian's full KYC, and proof of the guardian-minor relationship (or a court order for a legal guardian). The guardian uses standard adult modalities.
Tax overlayThe minor's investment income is generally clubbed with the income of the parent with the higher income, with only a small per-child exemption, until majority. After eighteen it is taxed in the now-adult's own hands. The tax event therefore does not land on the folio holder during minority.
Operating authorityGuardian, until majority.
Holding modeTypically single holding in the minor's name; a minor generally cannot be a joint holder. SOA or, for equities, a minor demat.
Banking and paymentMinor or minor-with-guardian account; resident rails, operated by guardian.
Nomination and transmissionGuardian arrangements apply during minority; nomination rules follow the holding form.
ReportingAs resident, with clubbing reflected in the parent's reporting during minority.
Special attention and opensMinor Attaining Majority (MAM) is a lifecycle event, not an edge case. On the minor turning eighteen the account is frozen for transactions until the erstwhile minor completes their own KYC, supplies their PAN and a fresh bank mandate, at which point the guardian's authority ceases and the tax subject changes identity. This transition is detailed in Part V.

NRI: Non-Resident Indian

Definition and attributesAn Indian passport holder who is non-resident under FEMA. The defining attributes are non-resident FEMA status, a country of residence, and the NRE/NRO/FCNR account framework that determines repatriability.
Instrument eligibilityMF and FD are available, the latter through NRE, NRO, or FCNR. Equities run through the PIS route (repatriable, NRE) or the non-PIS route (non-repatriable, NRO), delivery-based, with sectoral-cap exclusions and restrictions on intraday and certain derivatives. Bonds are mixed: corporate bonds and many government securities are open under the FEMA debt route, but fresh Sovereign Gold Bonds are barred for NRIs. SIF eligibility is genuinely unsettled (see opens).
KYCPAN is mandatory; Aadhaar is not, and many NRIs lack it, which has a direct status consequence below. Documents add the passport, overseas and any Indian address proof, visa or work permit, and an NRE/NRO bank proof; overseas documents typically need attestation and foreign-language documents a certified English translation. Video IPV is the major enabler, removing the need to fly in.
Tax overlayThe headline rates match a resident's, but the machinery differs. TDS is withheld at source on every redemption (equity short-term at 20 percent, equity long-term typically at 12.5 percent, higher for non-equity),6 rather than self-assessed. NRE and FCNR deposit interest is exempt with no TDS; NRO deposit interest is fully taxable with 30 percent TDS plus surcharge and cess. DTAA relief can reduce withholding, on production of a TRC and Form 10F. The basic-exemption-absorption privilege is not available to NRIs for Section 112A gains.
Operating authoritySelf, frequently through a Power-of-Attorney holder resident in India.
Holding modeSingle or joint; SOA or demat; repatriable or non-repatriable, following the funding account, which is the dominant attribute of every NRI holding.
Banking and paymentNRE, NRO, or FCNR only; eNACH on these accounts; ordinary domestic UPI from a foreign number is generally unavailable.
Nomination and transmissionAs for residents, with repatriability of transmitted proceeds following the original account classification.
ReportingHeavy: FATCA/CRS, at-source TDS with certificates, and Forms 15CA/15CB on repatriation. Country of residence drives DTAA and CRS.
Special attention and opens

Resolved: the relaxation lapsed; Validated is required (D2)

Because Validated status needs PAN-Aadhaar database validation and many NRIs lack Aadhaar, NRIs commonly cannot reach Validated and sit at Registered. SEBI's relaxation treating NRI/OCI records in Registered status as Validated for transacting across fund houses ran first to 30 April 2025, was extended to 30 April 2026, and lapsed on that date with no successor notified. NRI and OCI records must therefore reach Validated to transact. Confirmed as D2 in the decisions register and applied across the journey layer: J1 gates non-resident onboarding on it, and J9 carries it on redemption.

14.Non-individual entities

For every entity, the shape is constant: the entity holds its own PAN and KYC record, and separately the authorised signatories are KYC'd and the Ultimate Beneficial Owners are traced and verified. The authorising resolution and the signatory KYC are where onboarding actually slows down. Part IV
Cross-cutting modes and overlays

15.Joint holding

Joint holding is a mode, not a persona, and conflating the two is a common modelling error. Legally there is no joint investor entity; there are two or more natural-person personas holding one folio or account together. The mode is a configuration layered on top of those personas.

Key principle

Model joint as a relationship over personas, not as an investor type. The legal identity that matters for KYC, tax attribution, and transmission remains each individual holder. Treating joint as a first-class persona is a defensible operational shortcut given how prevalent joint folios are, but the shortcut must not leak into the places where individual identity is what counts.

Semantics that must be captured.

  • Holder order: a primary (first) holder and one or more secondary holders. Communications and statements default to the first holder.
  • Operation mode: Jointly, Either or Survivor, Anyone or Survivor, or Former or Survivor, which feeds the authorisation engine in Section 7.
  • Tax attribution: income and gains are generally attributed to the first holder, which means the joint mode does not split the tax, it assigns it.
  • Transmission: survivorship governs and takes precedence over a nominee. On a holder's death the holding vests in the survivors per the operation mode.
  • KYC: every holder must be individually KYC-valid; a joint folio is only as clear as its least-cleared holder.

A minor generally cannot be a joint holder, and an NRI joined with a resident raises repatriability and reporting questions that follow the first holder's status. Joint mode therefore interacts with almost every other facet, which is why it earns a section of its own rather than a line in each profile.

16.Accredited Investor status

Accreditation is a SEBI qualification status that can attach to an individual, HUF, trust, or body corporate meeting prescribed net-worth or income thresholds. It is an overlay carried as a flag on a persona, not a persona itself. Its most relevant effect in scope is that an accredited investor is exempt from the SIF minimum-ticket floor.

Open: verify current thresholds with Compliance

The exact net-worth and income thresholds for accreditation, and the accrediting-agency process, are specific figures that move. They are deliberately not quoted here. Confirm the current thresholds and the validity period of an accreditation certificate with Compliance before any flow relies on the status.

Part V
The persona lifecycle state model

A persona is not static. It moves between states, and each transition changes several facets at once. This is the dimension a pure lookup table misses, and the one most likely to become a painful retrofit if it is not modelled as events from the start.

The transitions below are best emitted as domain events, because each fans out into KYC, banking, tax, authority, and reporting consequences. The table states, for each transition, what triggers it and what changes.

TransitionTriggerWhat changes
Minor → Major (MAM) The minor turns eighteen. Account frozen for transactions until the erstwhile minor completes their own KYC and supplies their PAN and a fresh bank mandate. Guardian authority ceases. The tax subject changes identity, and clubbing with the parent stops. A mid-transition freeze sits in the middle of this, so it is a multi-step state, not an instant flip.
Resident → Non-Resident FEMA residential status changes on departure and the qualifying-period tests being met. Re-KYC with non-resident documents; resident bank accounts must be redesignated or converted to NRO and new NRE/FCNR opened; existing holdings reclassify; tax treatment and at-source TDS change; FATCA/CRS updates; country of residence becomes a live attribute.
Non-Resident → Resident Return to India and the residency tests being met. The reverse: account redesignation, removal of at-source TDS on MF, recovery of resident privileges, FATCA/CRS update. Often overlooked because platforms build the outbound case and forget the return.
Individual death → Transmission Death of a holder. Single holding transmits to the nominee, who must then KYC; joint holding vests in survivors per the operation mode, ahead of any nominee. A lifecycle event, not an exception path.
HUF partition Partition of the family. The HUF as an entity may cease or split; holdings are apportioned; PAN and tax status of the resulting parties change.
Entity dissolution / merger Winding up, dissolution, or merger of a company, LLP, firm, trust, or society. Terminal or transformative event for the holding; authority and PAN of the surviving or successor entity govern thereafter.
KYC status transition Validation, expiry of a relaxation window, or a document lapsing. Movement between On Hold, Registered, and Validated changes what the investor may do right now, including, at the On Hold end, blocking redemptions.
Accreditation gain / loss Meeting or ceasing to meet thresholds, or certificate expiry. Toggles access to accredited-only treatment such as the SIF minimum-ticket exemption.

Special attention

Two transitions are routinely under-built. The first is the return leg of residency (Non-Resident → Resident), because teams build the departure case and forget the homecoming. The second is the mid-transition freeze inside MAM, where the account is neither fully a minor's nor yet the adult's and must refuse transactions cleanly rather than fail obscurely. Both deserve explicit states, not implicit handling.

Part VIII
The build-scope view for an MFD-only firm

Everything above is persona-neutral: it describes what an investor is, irrespective of who is serving them. This part is the one place the document takes a stance. It answers a narrower question for a wealth-management firm operating under a distributor (MFD) licence, serving the mass-affluent and private-wealth segments: of all the elements catalogued above, which must be built as proper product flows, and which are there to be understood rather than plumbed.

18.The build-scope view

The principle that classifies almost everything

An MFD-only firm is not the system of record. The AMC and its RTA are. Units, money movement, tax withholding, statutory filings, and the legal transfer of holdings all happen on AMC and RTA rails. What the firm owns is the investor relationship, order origination, and servicing. The firm's own ledger is a reconciliation and reporting projection of the RTA's truth, not a replacement for it. The test for whether an element needs a real flow is therefore simple: does it sit in onboarding, order construction, mandate and payment orchestration, or servicing? If yes, build it. If it is execution, withholding, or filing that legally belongs to the AMC or RTA, integrate with it, reflect it, and at most facilitate it.

Reading the three buckets

Core build Own the flow end to end. Facilitate Build a servicing or initiation flow; the AMC or RTA owns the execution and the record. Referential Understand it, optionally surface it; no plumbing or obligation.

Two questions that anchor the split

Is tax something an MFD must build? No. Because the firm never touches the money, it is never the payer in the withholding sense. For residents there is no TDS on mutual fund redemptions at all; the investor self-assesses. For NRIs there is at-source TDS, but the AMC and RTA withhold it and issue the certificate, not the firm. The firm builds no investor tax-withholding and no tax-filing flow. Tax is referential: needed to understand for suitability and to set NRI expectations, and optionally surfaced as capital-gains and tax-lot reporting as a value-add for the segment, which consumes the RTA's data and is a business decision rather than a compliance obligation. One guardrail given the distributor posture: surfacing tax information is acceptable, charging for tax advice is not.

Whose job is post-death transfer? It splits. Capturing the nominee is the firm's to build, registered with the RTA at onboarding or first transaction. The post-death transfer is not the firm's to execute: the units move on the RTA's register against a transmission request and documents from the nominee or heir. The firm's role is to facilitate, by guiding the family, collecting and verifying documents, and routing to the RTA. For private wealth that facilitation is one of the most important relationship moments there is, so it should be built well, but built as a servicing-and-routing flow, not as a transfer engine.

Persona scope is segment-dependent

Not every persona earns a build, and which ones do splits by segment.

The mass-affluent core is Resident Individual, Minor, NRI, OCI, and Joint holding. That set, plus the core-build rows above, is most of a real platform. NRI and OCI carry the heaviest incremental build (banking, repatriability, the US/Canada gate, attestation-aware KYC), and they are disproportionately present in affluent books, so they are not a later nicety for this segment.

Private wealth is what pulls the entities into scope: HUF, Company, LLP, and the Private or Family Trust, because family offices, holding companies, and family trusts are how that money is actually held. Entity onboarding is heavy (UBO graphs, resolutions, deed-reading), so it is a deliberate build, but for the private-wealth book it is meaningful rather than academic.

The genuinely deferrable or referential personas are Foreign National resident in India, Public/Charitable Trust, Society/AOP/BOI, and Sole Proprietorship, the last of which collapses into the individual in any case.

The stripped-down core

Reduced to what an MFD-only firm must bake in, the build is: onboarding and KYC with status gating, the documentation and UBO backbone, operating authority, holding mode, payment and mandate orchestration, ARN and EUIN attribution, nominee capture, the reconciliation ledger, light suitability, and the MAM and residency state-handling. Everything tax, every statutory filing, and the legal execution of transmission and residency changes is integrate-reflect-facilitate, not build. The home-jurisdiction material is referential except for one sharp build, the US/Canada gate.

Special attention

The trap is the opposite of over-building. Treating transmission and residency change as pure AMC problems and shipping nothing is a real failure mode, because for private wealth those facilitation flows are exactly where the relationship is won or lost. "Facilitate" must not quietly become "ignore".

Open: verify against the chosen execution rail

The precise facilitate-versus-execute line on transmission and residency change can shift depending on whether the firm transacts direct to the RTA, through an exchange platform, or through a unified utility, since each rail hands over a different amount of the servicing. The principle holds across all three; the exact document and routing mechanics should be confirmed against whichever rail is settled on.
