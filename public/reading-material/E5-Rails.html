Rails and Counterparties

The external parties that execute and record, what each is the system of record for, how the firm integrates with it, and where it fails. The map every build-scope part in the suite leans on.

What this document is

A reference map of the external parties a distribution platform depends on: the counterparties that keep the authoritative records, and the rails that carry orders, money, and identity between the firm and those records. For each it states what truth the party owns, how the firm integrates with it, and how it fails. It is the lightest document in the suite by design, because its job is to be a clear map rather than a deep treatment, and it is the document the build-scope parts of the other four references quietly assume.

What it is not

Not a PRD, not a build plan, and not a vendor selection. It names the external ecosystem an MFD must integrate, by role and by the real institutions that play those roles, but it does not specify which commercial provider the firm should pick for any leg. The mechanics that ride these rails live in the other references and are pointed to, not repeated.

Scope

In scope: the fund-side counterparties (AMC, RTA, custodian, trustee); the demat-side counterparty (the depositories) and the clearing corporations behind exchange-routed orders; the order-routing rails; the payment rails; and the identity rails. For each, the truth it owns, the interface, the firm's role, and the failure modes.

Deliberately elsewhere: the order lifecycle and the mandate machinery live in the Order reference; the KYC status regime and the tax engine live in the Investor reference; holding form, nomination, and transmission live in the Folio reference. This document names the parties those mechanics run on and points back to each reference for the detail.

The organising principle: integrate through an adapter, never own

The firm is not the system of record for any of the truths below. It reflects each one, reconciles against it, and fronts the investor relationship, but the authoritative record always sits with an external party. The discipline that keeps this clean is to integrate each external truth through its own anti-corruption-layer adapter, one per domain, so the external party's model and quirks never leak into the firm's own model. An adapter translates the outside world into the firm's language and absorbs change on the outside without corrupting the inside. This single stance, paired with the not-the-system-of-record principle from the other references, classifies the entire build-scope view in Part V: the firm builds adapters and reconciliation, and treats every external record as referential.

Part I
The counterparty and rail model

1.What a counterparty and a rail are here

Two kinds of external party matter. A counterparty keeps an authoritative record, a truth the firm must reflect but does not own. A rail carries something between the firm and a counterparty: an order, a payment, or an identity check. The first group answers "who owns this truth"; the second answers "how does it move".

The distinction is not academic. The firm's relationship to a truth-keeper is to reconcile against it and never contradict it. The firm's relationship to a rail is to choose it, integrate it, and handle its failures. Mixing the two, treating a rail as if it owned a truth, or treating a record-keeper as if it were just a pipe, is the source of most integration confusion.

Key principle

Every truth in this domain has exactly one owner, and it is never the firm. The unit register is the RTA's, the NAV is the AMC's, the demat holding is the depository's, the KYC status is the registry's, the money in flight is the rail's and the bank's. The firm's job is to hold a reconciled projection of each and to move cleanly between them. Name the owner of every truth explicitly, because the build-scope view is just that map with a badge attached to each row.

There is a third structural fact about how these parties are reached. An MFD rarely integrates each rail in this document directly. A market of infrastructure providers sits between the firm and the rails, wrapping the transaction, payment, identity, and data rails behind unified APIs, so the firm typically integrates a small number of aggregating providers rather than every counterparty one by one.

Special attention: the rails are often consumed through an abstraction layer

A third-party abstraction layer is, in effect, someone else's anti-corruption layer: the firm trusts a vendor's translation of the external truth rather than writing its own adapter against the source. That can be the right call, but it cuts against the one-adapter-per-domain discipline in two ways. First, a single provider often spans more than one domain, so the clean domain boundary the firm wants on the inside does not match the provider boundary on the outside. Second, the firm inherits the provider's failure modes and model, not just the rail's. The discipline still holds: keep the firm's own domain adapter even when a provider already abstracts the rail, so the provider can be swapped without its model having leaked inside. The provider abstracts the rail; the firm's adapter abstracts the provider.

The providers cluster by the rail they abstract. Named by function rather than by vendor, the principal layers are:

  • Transaction and settlement infrastructure. Wraps the order-routing rails and the RTA transaction processing behind one API: order placement across the transaction family, SIP and mandate registration, folio creation, and the allotment and settlement callbacks, through a single integration rather than one per platform.
  • Payments and mandates. Wraps the bank and NPCI rails and the payment-aggregator path: collection, mandate setup and the pre-debit lifecycle, and money-leg reconciliation, always subject to the no-pooling constraint that keeps the firm out of the flow of funds.
  • Identity, KYC, and onboarding. Wraps the KRA, CKYC, Aadhaar, and PAN sources: document capture, face match and liveness, video KYC, registry fetch, and PAN-Aadhaar validation, behind one onboarding flow.
  • Holdings data and consolidation. Wraps the RTA mailback files, the consolidated account statement, and the servicing portals into a unified holdings and reconciliation feed for the firm's own book and for securities held away within mutual funds and demat. The broader cross-institution picture comes through the consent rail below.
  • Consent-based financial data (Account Aggregator). Wraps the RBI-regulated Account Aggregator framework, through which a user consents to share held-away financial data, bank and deposit balances, investments, insurance, and more, drawn from the institutions that hold it. This is the rail behind a consolidated net-worth or financial-picture view that reaches beyond the firm's own book, with consent that is explicit, purpose-limited, time-bound, and revocable by the user.

This document names no providers on purpose. The set is a competitive, fast-changing market, and which provider wraps which rail is a build-and-vendor decision the suite leaves out. What belongs here is that the layer exists, the five functions it splits into, and the discipline it demands.

Each party in Parts II and III is described through the same five facets, so they read side by side:

  3. Interface: how the firm integrates with it.
  4. The firm's role: what the firm does with or through it.
  5. Failure modes: how it breaks, and what the firm must design around.

Part II
The truth-keepers

The parties that own an authoritative record. The fund chain is described in depth in the Instrument reference; here it is mapped for its record-ownership and failure modes.

4.The fund chain: AMC, RTA, custodian, trustee

AMC (Asset Management Company)

What it isThe company that runs a mutual fund or SIF scheme and makes its investment decisions.
What truth it ownsThe scheme and its NAV. The AMC strikes the NAV each valuation day; that price is the AMC's truth, consumed by everyone downstream.
InterfaceLargely indirect for an MFD. The firm reaches the AMC through the RTA or an order-routing rail, and consumes the AMC's published NAV and scheme data.
The firm's roleReflect the scheme in the catalog, consume the NAV, and surface AMC-driven events. The firm originates orders against the scheme but does not transact with the AMC directly.
Failure modesA scheme closed to fresh purchase, a change in fundamental attributes that triggers an exit window, side-pocketing on a credit event, a scheme merger, an NFO timing constraint, or a delayed or restated NAV. Each is an event the firm must reflect and route, not control.

RTA (Registrar and Transfer Agent: CAMS, KFin)

What it isThe registrar that maintains the official records for an AMC's schemes. Two RTAs serve the industry: CAMS, handling roughly two-thirds of industry AUM, and KFin, handling the rest.1
What truth it ownsThe unit register: the authoritative record of who holds how many units in SOA form, the folio, the nominee, and the lien. It also processes the transaction itself, the allotment, the settlement, the transmission, and the nomination registration. This is the single most important external truth in the whole map.
InterfaceTransactions route to the RTA either directly or through an order-routing rail. Holdings and reconciliation come back separately, through daily mailback files, not through the transaction rail. The two flows are distinct, and the firm needs both.1
The firm's roleReflect the folio and the holding, reconcile the firm's projection against the RTA mailback files, and front the investor for everything the RTA executes. The firm never holds the register; it mirrors it.
Failure modes

Red flag: the RTA is the central single dependency

Because the RTA owns the register and executes the transaction, an RTA outage or processing delay halts the firm's ability to transact and to show a current holding, regardless of how robust the firm's own systems are. Add the not-in-good-order rejection (a transaction bounced for a data mismatch), folio mismatches, and reconciliation breaks between the mailback file and the firm's view, and the RTA integration is the one whose failure modes most need explicit, visible handling rather than silent retries.

Custodian

What it isThe institution that holds the underlying securities the scheme owns.
What truth it ownsThe scheme's assets. This is a truth about the fund's holdings, not about the investor's units.
InterfaceNone, for an MFD. The custodian sits inside the fund's operations and is not a party the distributor integrates with.
The firm's roleUnderstand that it exists and what it secures. Nothing to build.
Failure modesNot investor-facing for a distributor. Purely referential.

Trustee

What it isThe body that holds the scheme in trust for investors and oversees the AMC.
What truth it ownsNo data record the firm consumes. It owns oversight, a governance function.
InterfaceNone.
The firm's roleUnderstand the governance structure. Nothing to integrate.
Failure modesNot an operational dependency for the firm. Referential.

5.The depositories and clearing corporations

Depository (NSDL, CDSL)

What it isThe institution that holds securities in electronic form. India has two, NSDL and CDSL, reached through a depository participant.
What truth it ownsThe demat holding: the authoritative record of dematerialised units and securities, across all AMCs and asset classes, and the pledge or lien marked on them.
InterfaceThrough a depository participant. An MFD is not itself a depository participant, so it reflects a demat account it does not keep.
The firm's roleReflect demat holdings where the investor holds in that form, and read the standardised pledge and lien state the depository exposes. The depository becomes central only when equities enter scope, since equities can only be held in demat.
Failure modesDepository or participant downtime, delivery-instruction and pledge-processing delays, and beneficial-owner mismatches against the firm's view.

Clearing corporation

What it isThe settlement body behind an exchange. Exchange-routed mutual fund orders settle through the exchange's clearing corporation rather than through the distributor.
What truth it ownsThe settlement of an exchange-routed order: it stands between the investor's payment and the AMC so the money never rests with the distributor.
InterfaceIndirect, through the exchange order-routing rail the firm uses.
The firm's roleUnderstand that the clearing corporation is how the no-pooling rule is satisfied on the exchange rail, and reconcile against the settlement it effects.
Failure modesSettlement delays and exchange-side processing failures, surfaced through the routing rail.

Special attention

The clearing corporation is the quiet reason the exchange-routing rails comply with the SEBI no-pooling rule. When an order routes through an exchange platform, the money flows investor to clearing corporation to AMC, never through the distributor's hands. This is why the choice of order-routing rail and the choice of money path are entangled: the rail decides who sits between the investor's bank and the fund, and therefore how the non-pooling constraint is met.

Part III
The conduits

The rails that carry orders, money, and identity. These are the parts the firm actively chooses, integrates, and handles the failures of.

6.Order-routing rails

An order can reach the RTA by more than one path, and the path shapes what the firm must build and how much of the servicing it has to handle. There are three live transaction rails plus the direct route, and a separate servicing portal.

RailWhat it isNote
BSE StAR MFThe exchange MF platform run by BSE.The largest by volume, processing tens of millions of transactions a month; most back-office providers integrate with it.2
NSE NMF IIThe exchange MF platform run by NSE.Known for a more modern API; settles through the NSE clearing chain.2
MFU (MF Utilities)The industry-owned shared utility, promoted by AMFI.Gives the investor a Common Account Number (CAN) spanning participating AMCs, and a consolidated payment. Popular with individual distributors.2
RTA-directIntegration straight to CAMS and KFin.No exchange or utility in between; the firm carries more of the servicing itself.
MF CentralThe investor servicing portal jointly run by CAMS and KFin.A consolidated-view and servicing rail, not primarily a distributor order-routing rail. Useful for held-away and servicing flows.2

Open: the routing-rail choice is a real fork, confirm it internally

The Investor and Order references both noted that the facilitate-versus-execute line on servicing shifts with the rail, because each hands over a different amount of the work. An exchange platform brings its clearing corporation and more of the settlement plumbing; the RTA-direct route leaves more for the firm to do but is closer to the source of truth; the utility route brings the CAN and consolidated payment. Which rail or rails the firm integrates first is an integration decision the suite deliberately leaves open. Confirm it before the order-routing adapter is built, because it changes the shape of that adapter.

7.Payment rails

The money leg runs on several rails, and they are governed by both an RBI regime and a SEBI constraint that interact. The mechanics, the cut-off and realisation rules, the mandate lifecycle, and the transaction limits, live in the Order reference; here the rails are mapped as parties.

RailOwned or run byWhat it carries
UPI and UPI autopayNPCIOne-time pulls and recurring mandates for smaller and mid-size tickets, within the capital-markets limits set out in the Order reference.
NACH and eNACHNPCIThe standing-instruction rail behind SIPs, under the RBI e-mandate framework detailed in the Order reference.
NEFT, RTGS, IMPS, netbankingThe banks, on RBI railsOne-time transfers, carrying the large tickets that exceed the digital-rail ceilings, including the first SIF purchase.
The payment aggregator pathA payment aggregator under RBI authorisationThe mediated path that moves money from the investor's bank toward the AMC where a gateway sits in the flow.

The two regimes that govern the money leg.

The RBI overhauled the payment-aggregator regime in 2025. The Reserve Bank of India (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, 2025, dated 15 September 2025, consolidated the earlier guidelines into one master direction, classified aggregators into online, physical, and cross-border categories, set non-bank net-worth thresholds, and hardwired an escrow architecture so that merchant and customer funds sit in a ring-fenced account with a scheduled commercial bank.3 Above that sits the SEBI constraint the whole suite is built around: a distributor must not pool investor money.4 The two meet at the payment rail.

Red flag: the money path must satisfy SEBI no-pooling, whichever rail is chosen

The RBI aggregator regime governs how a payment aggregator handles funds in escrow; the SEBI no-pooling rule governs whether the distributor may ever sit in the money flow. They are different constraints, and both must be met. On the exchange rails the clearing corporation satisfies no-pooling; on a payment-aggregator path the design must ensure the money reaches the AMC without the distributor pooling it. Confirm that whichever money path is chosen keeps the firm out of the flow of funds, because this is the constraint that most shapes the payment architecture.

8.Identity rails

Identity and KYC ride a small set of registries and sources. The KYC status regime and its consequences live in the Investor reference; here the rails are mapped as parties.

RailWhat it isWhat it owns
KRA (CVL, NDML, CAMS, KFin)The KYC Registration Agencies for the securities market.The securities KYC record and its status (Validated, Registered, On-Hold).
CKYC at CERSAIThe Central KYC Registry maintained by CERSAI.The fourteen-digit cross-sector KYC identifier spanning banking, insurance, and securities.
UIDAIThe Aadhaar authority.Aadhaar identity, behind OTP and biometric eKYC.
Income Tax DepartmentThe PAN authority and validation source.PAN, and the PAN-Aadhaar linkage that determines whether a PAN is operative.

Resolved: the NRI and OCI KRA-status relaxation, carried from the Investor reference

Because Validated status needs PAN-Aadhaar database validation that many NRIs lack, NRI and OCI records commonly sit at Registered. The relaxation letting Registered-status records transact across fund houses lapsed on 30 April 2026 with no successor notified, so non-resident onboarding must reach Validated. Confirmed as D2 in the decisions register and grounded in J1; the Investor reference carries the full detail.

Special attention

The identity rails are where the firm's own work is heaviest, even though the records are external. The firm captures, runs the modality, consumes the status, and gates orders on it, while the KRA, CERSAI, UIDAI, and the tax database own the underlying truth. The failure modes to design around are a fetch failure, a validation still pending, a status downgrade, an eKYC outage, and a PAN turned inoperative by a lapsed Aadhaar linkage, each of which can block a transaction at the pre-flight check the Order reference describes.

Part V
The build-scope view

The lightest build-scope view in the suite, because the answer is almost uniform: integrate through the adapter, never own. The only genuine build here is the adapters and the reconciliation around them; every external record is referential, and the servicing that runs through these parties is facilitate.

Core build Own end to end. Facilitate Assist; the external party executes. Referential Understand; no plumbing.
ElementBucketWhy, and who executes

The stripped-down core

Reduced to what an MFD-only firm must build at the rail layer, there is one pattern: an adapter per external domain, reconciliation against each owner's record, and disciplined failure handling at each adapter. Everything else is reflect and facilitate. No truth in this document is the firm's to own, which is exactly why the discipline is the adapter: it is the boundary that keeps the external world's models and outages from leaking into a system that must present one coherent view to the investor.

10.Glossary

Plain definitions of the rail and counterparty terms a reader without a finance background may want unpacked. Terms defined in the earlier references (RTA, NAV, SOA, demat account, depository, DP, BO, eNACH, NACH, UPI autopay) are not repeated.

TermPlain meaning
Account Aggregator (AA)An RBI-regulated intermediary that lets a user consent to share their financial data from the institutions that hold it to a service that needs it, on a purpose-limited, time-bound, and revocable basis. The rail behind a cross-institution financial-picture or net-worth view.
Anti-corruption layer (ACL)An adapter that sits between the firm's own model and an external system, translating between the two so the outside system's quirks and changes do not leak into and corrupt the firm's model.
CAN (Common Account Number)A single account number from the MF Utilities platform that lets an investor transact across many fund houses through one identity and one consolidated payment.
CERSAIThe central registry that maintains the Central KYC Registry (CKYC), among other records.
CKYCThe Central KYC record and its fourteen-digit identifier, shared across banking, insurance, and securities.
Clearing corporationThe settlement body behind an exchange, which stands between buyer and seller so money and securities change hands safely. For exchange-routed mutual fund orders it keeps the money out of the distributor's hands.
Escrow accountA ring-fenced bank account in which a payment aggregator holds funds it is moving, kept separate from its own money under RBI rules.
KRA (KYC Registration Agency)A SEBI-registered agency that stores and shares an investor's securities-market KYC record and status. CVL, NDML, CAMS, and KFin are the KRAs.
Mailback fileThe daily data file an RTA sends a distributor with holdings and transaction details, the channel through which the firm reconciles, separate from the order-routing channel.
MF CentralThe investor servicing and consolidated-view portal jointly run by the two RTAs, CAMS and KFin.
MFU (MF Utilities)The industry-owned shared transaction utility, promoted by AMFI, that issues the Common Account Number.
Payment aggregator (PA)A regulated entity that sits in a payment flow to collect money on behalf of a merchant and settle it onward, under the RBI's aggregator directions.
UIDAIThe authority that issues Aadhaar and runs the identity checks behind Aadhaar-based eKYC.
