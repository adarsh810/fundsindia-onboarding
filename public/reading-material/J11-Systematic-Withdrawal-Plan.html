J11: Systematic Withdrawal Plan

The recurring money-out plan: the withdrawal-side mirror of the SIP, drawn as J8's plan lifecycle running J9's redemption spine on each scheduled date. It needs no payment mandate, because money flows outward, and an instalment simply does not generate when the units are not there.

What this document is

The governing view of how a Systematic Withdrawal Plan runs. An SWP is a long-lived standing instruction that, on each scheduled date, sells units back to the fund and pays the proceeds to the investor's bank account. It is the withdrawal-side mirror of the SIP, and it borrows two flows wholesale rather than redrawing them. The plan lifecycle, Registered through Active to Completed or Cancelled, and the two-clocks rule that keeps a single instalment from moving the plan, are J8's, reused unchanged. The per-instalment flow, the redemption itself with its unencumbered-units gate, its exit-load haircut, and the residential-status fork that withholds TDS at source for an NRI and routes proceeds by repatriability, is J9's, run once per scheduled date. What J11 owns is the joint between them, and the one thing that makes the withdrawal plan genuinely different from the investment plan: it carries no payment mandate, so a scheduled date with too few units does not fail, it simply produces nothing.

What it is not

Not a reference and not a PRD. It does not redraw the redemption order, which is J9 instantiated each cycle, and it does not redraw the plan lifecycle, which is J8. It is the recurring, one-scheme, money-out plan specifically. The recurring two-scheme money-out, the STP, is J10, which reuses the same plan pattern but moves between schemes inside one AMC; the one-time money-out is J9. Both are out of scope here. Because the SWP has no bank debit, the mandate machinery the SIP leans on, the registration in J5, the per-cycle pre-debit notification, the pause-on-failed-debit policy, does not apply in the same way, and saying exactly where the mirror breaks is part of what this draft is for.

1.The spine

An SWP has the same two altitudes as a SIP, and keeping them apart is again the whole craft. There is the plan, a long-lived instruction registered once and living until its tenure ends or the investor stops it. And there is the instalment, a fresh child redemption minted on each scheduled date that gates, prices, and pays out on its own. The first diagram draws the plan lifecycle, which is J8's exactly, minus the mandate. The second draws what one scheduled date does, which is a J9 redemption with one gate moved to the front. Neither redraws the redemption spine itself, which each instalment runs in full.

The plan lifecycle

flowchart TD
  A([New SWP intent, a scheme in a folio, amount or units, schedule and tenure]) --> B{The folio holds redeemable units to draw from}
  B -->|yes| C[Register the plan, schedule and amount or units and tenure, no mandate]
  B -->|no holding to draw from| X1([Blocked, there is nothing to withdraw])
  C --> D([Active, instalments generate on schedule])
  D -->|on each scheduled date| E[Attempt a child redemption that runs the J9 spine]
  E -->|outcome recorded, plan unchanged by one result| D
  D -->|tenure or instalment count reached, or the holding is exhausted| G([Completed])

The plan is Registered once its schedule, its amount or unit quantity, and its tenure are set, and unlike a SIP it links no mandate, because nothing is being debited. It turns Active when instalments begin generating, and from there it loops, attempting a redemption each scheduled date and returning to Active regardless of how any single instalment turned out, until it reaches Completed at the end of its tenure or instalment count, or earlier if the holding runs out. Two states sit off this happy path and are reached deliberately: Paused, the investor suspending the plan, bulleted in Section 4, and Cancelled, the investor stopping it before its natural end. Cancelling stops future instalments only; it never reaches back to claw proceeds that already settled. The plan lifecycle and the two-clocks principle below are J8's, reused without change.

What one scheduled date does

flowchart TD
  A([Scheduled date reached]) --> B{Available unencumbered units cover this instalment}
  B -->|no, too few free units| K([Non-generation, nothing emitted, plan stays Active, recorded not failed])
  B -->|yes| C[Emit a child redemption order that runs the J9 spine, gates, NAV, exit load, TDS fork, payout]
  C --> D{Instalment outcome}
  D -->|settled| G([Instalment Settled, proceeds paid out, plan continues])
  D -->|payout-tail failure after allotment| H([Instalment Reversed in J9, plan continues])

The single change from a SIP's scheduled date is the front gate. Where a SIP asks whether the mandate can be debited, an SWP asks whether the folio holds enough free, unencumbered units to satisfy the instalment. If it does, a child redemption is emitted and runs the J9 spine in full, including the residential-status TDS fork and the repatriability routing per instalment. If it does not, the instalment simply does not generate: nothing is emitted, the plan stays Active, and the event is recorded rather than treated as a failure. That distinction, between a non-event and a failure, is the one genuine decision J11 surfaces, drawn in Section 2. Both real outcomes, a settled instalment and a payout-tail reversal, return the plan to Active; the plan does not flinch at one result either way.

Who acts at each step

StepActing party (by role)What the firm does

Key principle: two entities, two clocks, reused from J8

The plan and the instalment are separate entities with separate lifecycles, and the same discipline J8 owns governs the SWP. The plan carries the schedule, the amount or units, and the start and end conditions; each instalment carries a gate, a price, a haircut, and a payout. A single instalment outcome, whether a clean settlement, a payout-tail reversal, or a scheduled date that produced nothing, must not move the plan by itself. J11 inherits this clock and adds no new plan mechanics; it only changes what one scheduled date attempts, from a funded purchase to a gated redemption.

2.Decision points

One genuine question J11 owns and draws, and one inherited fork it runs per instalment but does not redraw.

Non-generation versus failure, and what it means for a pause threshold

This is the decision J11 exists to surface, and it is a refinement of the SIP's pause policy rather than a new question. In a SIP, a scheduled date where the debit bounces marks that instalment Failed and may count toward the consecutive-failure threshold that pauses the plan (D9). An SWP has no debit to bounce. When the units are insufficient, the Order reference is explicit that the instalment simply does not generate: there is no order, no rail attempt, and nothing to mark Failed. The product question is therefore whether a non-generating SWP instalment should count toward a pause threshold the way a failed SIP debit does.

flowchart TD
  P([Scheduled date, available units do not cover the instalment]) --> Q[Record a non-generation, no order emitted, plan stays Active]
  Q --> R{Should this count toward a pause threshold}
  R -->|recommended, no| S([Plan continues, next instalment attempts on schedule, investor optionally notified the holding is thin])
  R -->|alternative, yes| T([Treat repeated non-generation like repeated failure, pause and prompt])

Open: non-generation is a D9 refinement for Product, not a new row

The recommended reading follows the Order reference: a non-generation is recorded but is not a failure event, because no debit and no order exist to fail, so it should not feed the consecutive-failure pause the SIP uses. A thin or empty holding is better surfaced to the investor as information, the plan is approaching the end of what it can pay, than punished as if a debit had bounced, and an SWP that runs a holding to zero reaches Completed on its own. The alternative, treating sustained non-generation like sustained failure and pausing, is defensible if Product wants a single uniform plan-health rule across SIP and SWP. Either way this is a refinement to D9's scope, who and what the pause counts, not a separate decision; it is surfaced as a D9 note for Product, not a new register row. Owner: Product.

The per-instalment residential-status fork, inherited from J9

Each SWP instalment is a full redemption, so it carries J9's signature fork in full: for a resident the proceeds land whole and are self-assessed, while for an NRI or OCI the capital-gains TDS is withheld at source on that instalment and a certificate issued, with the net routed by the folio's repatriability tag to an NRE or NRO account. This is drawn in J9 and is not redrawn here; the altitude rule keeps it where it belongs. What J11 adds is only that it runs every cycle, so an NRI's SWP produces a withholding and a certificate on each scheduled date rather than once, which is the per-instalment brush of D45 noted in Section 6. The asymmetry with a SIP is worth stating plainly: a SIP's per-instalment overlay is on the way in, the mandate and its ceiling; an SWP's is on the way out, the TDS and repatriability of the payout.

3.Failure taxonomy

The ways an SWP breaks separate into the same two levels as a SIP, plan-level and instalment-level, but the instalment level looks different because there is no mandate. The dividing rule is the two-clocks principle: a single instalment outcome never moves the plan by itself.

How it breaksCauseWhere it lands, and recovery
Insufficient or encumbered unitsOn a scheduled date the free, unencumbered balance does not cover the instalment, because units are pledged, lien-marked, or simply fewer than the plan asks.Non-generation: no order is emitted and nothing is marked Failed. The plan stays Active and attempts again next cycle. This is the SWP's defining instalment-level event, and it is recorded, not a failure.
Below-minimum residualAn instalment that would leave a balance below the scheme minimum, or a fixed-units plan asking for more than remains.Handled per scheme rule, commonly by forcing that instalment to a full redemption of the residual rather than leaving an unviable balance, which can move the plan to Completed. Surface the choice rather than silently converting it. Inherited from J9.
Payout cannot landThe registered bank account is closed, frozen, or invalid when an instalment's proceeds are paid, after Allotted.A post-allotment failure on that instalment: units are gone but the money cannot land. Resolves by correcting the bank mandate and re-paying; if unwound it is a Reversed, not a Failed. The plan continues. Owned by the J9 and J6 spine.
Wrong repatriability or misapplied TDS on an instalmentAn NRI instalment paid to the wrong account class, or withheld at the wrong rate, on the J9 payout tail.The J9 red flag, now recurring: a payout-tail error that repeats every cycle if the routing is wrong once. Lands on the J9 spine; the plan-level consequence is that the defect compounds across instalments until corrected.
Holding exhausted under a live planThe plan has drawn the folio down to nothing or to a locked-only residual.Not a failure: the plan reaches Completed when there is nothing left it can redeem. A natural terminal the investor should see coming, not an error.
Plan cancelled by the investorThe investor stops the SWP before its tenure ends.The plan moves to Cancelled; future instalments stop. Settled instalments are untouched; there is no claw-back of proceeds already paid.

Red flag: a thin holding is a non-event, but a recurring payout-tail error is the real danger

Two things must not be confused. A scheduled date that produces nothing because the units are not there is a non-event: it must not be treated as a failure, must not pause the plan on its own, and must not trigger a retry that double-redeems when units later appear. The genuine danger is the J9 payout tail made recurring. Once a redemption is allotted the obligation to pay exists, so a wrong repatriability route or a misapplied TDS does not just harm one instalment, it repeats on every cycle until someone catches it. The SWP turns a single payout-tail defect into a standing one, which is the reason the routing must be right at registration, not patched per instalment.

4.Special cases

Variants that change a value or add a check without rerouting the plan lifecycle or the per-instalment redemption. Listed, never drawn.

  • Fixed-amount versus fixed-units. A fixed-rupee SWP sells whatever number of units the NAV implies on each date, so the units leaving vary with price; a fixed-units SWP sells a set quantity, so the rupee payout varies. A capture-and-conversion detail per instalment, not a lifecycle change, and the variant decides which residual rule in Section 3 bites first.
  • NRI TDS on every instalment. Each instalment withholds capital-gains TDS at source for an NRI or OCI and issues a certificate, so the investor should see the gross, the exit load, the TDS, and the net per cycle as distinct figures. The platform displays these; in V1 it reflects the TDS the RTA and AMC return rather than deriving it, holding no cost basis. This is the per-instalment brush of D45.
  • Exit-load bands and lock-in per instalment. The load and any lock-in apply to the specific units being sold on each date, following the scheme convention and the order of units, so early instalments may carry a load that later ones do not, and a locked tranche blocks only its own units. A value-and-eligibility detail checked per cycle.
  • DTAA relief for a non-resident. A Tax Residency Certificate plus a Form 10F can reduce the Indian withholding on an NRI's instalments. It changes the withholding rate, not the flow, and depends on the investor furnishing the documents. Inherited from J9.
  • Investor-initiated pause and resume. The investor suspends the plan deliberately and resumes later, the same Paused state J8 carries. No instalments generate while paused. Unlike a SIP there is no mandate to keep alive across the pause, which makes resume simpler.
  • No pre-debit notification, by design. The 24-hour pre-debit notification the SIP must send is a money-in, mandate-rail obligation. An SWP has no bank debit, so that specific notification does not apply. The firm may still notify before a payout as a courtesy or a control, but it is not the e-mandate framework's pre-debit alert, and J11 should not borrow that mechanic uncritically from J8.
  • Capital depletion and longevity. An SWP can outlive the holding it draws from. A fixed-rupee plan against a falling market depletes units faster, and the plan naturally tends toward Completed as the folio thins. This is a planning consequence the experience layer should make visible, not a lifecycle branch.
  • Repatriation certification. Remitting an NRI's NRO or sale proceeds abroad uses Forms 15CA and 15CB per remittance. A downstream servicing step that follows each payout, not a step inside the SWP spine. Inherited from J9.
  • Persona authority. A minor's SWP is operated by the guardian until majority, when the lifecycle freeze owned by J2 interrupts it; a joint folio redeems per its operation mode. These set who may register and run the plan, not how it runs.

5.Entities and states exercised

The references and journeys J11 draws on. Per the precedence rule J11 invents no state. It composes J8's plan clock with J9's redemption spine, drops the mandate, and changes one gate, and like both of them it finds no missing state.

Reference or journeyWhat J11 exercises
OrderThe SWP as a member of the transaction family, money out, one scheme, recurring, needing no mandate; the standing-instruction-spawns-child-order separation; the plan states Registered, Active, Paused, Completed, Cancelled; and the explicit rule that an instalment simply does not generate when the units are insufficient.
J9 (redemption)By reference: the full per-instalment flow, the unencumbered-units and lock-in gates, the exit-load haircut, the spine to a payout some working days after the NAV, and the residential-status fork that withholds TDS for an NRI and routes by repatriability.

InstrumentThe scheme attributes each instalment reads: the lock-in, the exit-load bands, and the cut-off and NAV mechanics every redemption inherits.

Key principle: J11 is a composition, and it confirms the rows it touches rather than adding any

J11 introduces no new entity and no new state. It is J8's plan running J9's redemption, and drawing it tested whether that composition holds without a mandate. It does: the plan clock, the non-generation behaviour, and the per-instalment TDS and repatriability all sit on states and rules the Order, Folio, and Investor references already carry. The one thing J11 sharpens is the scope of D9, by showing that a non-generating SWP instalment is structurally unlike a failed SIP debit. J11 adds no register row and flags no reference gap.

Open: what hardens J11

J11 is drawn against settled or applied decisions and the J8 and J9 spines it composes, so its lifecycle and gates are stable. The one thing it leaves open is the D9 refinement: Product needs to confirm that a non-generating instalment is recorded rather than counted toward a pause, and decide whether SIP and SWP share one plan-health rule or keep separate ones. The per-instalment NRI payout also keeps D45 live, since the platform still cannot derive the TDS itself in V1, but that is J9's call recurring rather than a new one. Closing the D9 refinement with Product is what moves J11 from decision-surfacing to governing. Nothing here blocks the wave.
