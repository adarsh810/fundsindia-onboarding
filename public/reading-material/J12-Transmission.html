J12: Transmission

The post-death transfer of units: classifying the folio, assembling the documentation the value demands, and letting the RTA effect the transfer folio by folio, while the firm facilitates and tracks but never adjudicates. The heaviest journey, drawn last, and the one place the contested nominee-versus-survivor-versus-heir boundary is exercised.

AudienceEngineering & Product

What this document is

The governing view of how a holding passes on the death of its holder. Transmission is not a sale and not an order; it is the transfer of a deceased investor's units to the surviving holder, the nominee, or the legal heirs, effected by the RTA on its register while the firm facilitates the paperwork and tracks the claim. J12 draws the spine, a death intimation, the classification of the folio into one of three paths, the documentation each path and value demands, and the RTA's folio-by-folio execution, then maps the ways it breaks and the cases that complicate it. It is the consumer of the nomination J4 captured and the freeze J4 hands over the moment a death is intimated. It draws on Folio for the transmission mechanics by holding type, on Investor for succession and authority, on Rails for the RTA as executor, and on J2 for the claimant's own KYC.

What it is not

Not a reference and not a PRD, and emphatically not an adjudicator. The firm facilitates and tracks; the RTA executes on its register; disputes between claimants are settled between them or in court, never by the platform. J12 carries the one genuinely contested decision in the suite, D12, the precedence between a nominee, a surviving holder, and the legal heirs, and it is drawn here against the register's recommended position with a Legal-pending callout, because the opinion has not yet landed. The persona set is the locked V1 four, so the undivided-family devolution is out of scope. One framing governs everything: a nominee or a surviving holder receives in trust, not as the final owner, so transmission is the moment the platform must be most careful never to present nomination as estate planning.

1.The spine

Transmission begins with an intimation, not a request, and it forks immediately on a single question: how was the folio held. A jointly held folio passes by survivorship and barely touches the nominee at all. A sole folio with a nominee passes to that nominee as a trustee. A sole folio with no nominee passes to the legal heirs on succession documentation, the heaviest path of the three. Whichever path it takes, the documentation steps up with the value, the RTA effects the transfer on its register folio by folio, and the new holder is free to set a fresh nomination on what they now hold. The firm's role across all of it is to capture, guide, and track, and never to decide who is entitled.

flowchart TD
  A([Death intimation received, folios where the deceased is the first holder are stop-marked]) --> B{Classify the folio by how it was held}
  B -->|jointly held with a survivor| C([Survivorship, the deceased is removed and the folio continues with the survivor, ahead of any nominee])
  B -->|sole holder with a registered nominee| D([Transmit to the nominee, who receives as a trustee for the legal heirs])
  B -->|sole holder with no nominee| E([Legal-heir path on succession documentation])
  C --> F[Assemble documentation per path and per the PAN-level value threshold]
  D --> F
  E --> F
  F --> G[The RTA effects the transmission on its register, folio by folio in SOA form]
  G --> H([Units transmit, the new holder may set a fresh nomination on the resulting holding])

The three paths differ sharply in weight. Survivorship is the lightest: the surviving holder submits a death certificate and the deceased is removed, with the folio continuing, and under the current framework no KYC, indemnity, or undertaking is sought from the surviving holder. The nominee path is moderate: the nominee submits a transmission request form, the death certificate, their own KYC, and a bank proof, with the documentation stepping up above the value threshold. The legal-heir path is the heaviest: with no nominee, succession documentation is required, and it escalates with value through affidavits, indemnities, and no-objections to, at the top band, a probated will or a letter of administration. The threshold that governs the step-up is computed at the PAN level, on the aggregate value of all the folios in the claim, not folio by folio, even though the RTA then executes each folio separately. That split, one threshold computed in aggregate but execution done per folio, is the structural subtlety of transmission.

Key principle: the firm facilitates and tracks, the RTA executes, and neither the platform nor the firm decides who is entitled

Every part of transmission that looks like a decision is either the RTA's execution or a legal question between claimants. The firm's contribution is to make the path obvious, collect the right documents the first time, track a claim that can stretch across many folios and several fund houses, and hand a dispute to legal escalation rather than resolving it. The Folio reference names the trap precisely: letting facilitate quietly become ignore. Transmission is where a private-wealth relationship is most tested, so the firm must build the tracking and the guidance even though it builds no register, and it must never cross the line into adjudicating who should receive.

2.Decision points

One contested legal boundary, drawn against the register's recommended position with the opinion still pending, and one documentation gate that steps up with value, tabled.

The nominee, survivor, and heir precedence (D12)

This is the signature decision of the journey and the one genuinely unsettled question in the suite. A nominee receives units on death so the family can reach them quickly, but holds them as a trustee, not as the final owner; the legal heirs under a will or succession law remain the rightful owners, and their claim can prevail where the two differ. On a joint folio, survivorship comes first and the nominee does not enter until the last holder dies. The fork below is drawn against the recommended position, not a settled rule.

flowchart TD
  P([A holding to transmit on death]) --> Q{Holding type and whether a claim is contested}
  Q -->|joint folio with a survivor| R([Survivor takes the holding ahead of any nominee, by survivorship])
  Q -->|nominee, no competing heir claim| S([Nominee receives as a trustee for the legal heirs])
  Q -->|nominee and heirs differ, or heirs contest| T([Heir-claim servicing path, beneficial rights can prevail, a dispute escalates to legal])

Open: the precedence is a Legal judgment still in flight (D12)

The recommended position J12 is drawn against is that the surviving holder and the nominee receive in trust, that the heirs' beneficial rights can prevail where they differ, and that the platform must therefore design for a heir-claim servicing path rather than treating the nominee as the final owner. This is the register's recommendation, not a settled rule: the Folio reference itself calls the boundary contested, and a Legal opinion is being sought from the week of 15 June and has not yet landed. Until it does, J12's precedence fork is provisional, and the firm's safe posture is the one drawn: receive to the nominee or survivor, never represent that as final ownership, and route any heir contest to legal escalation rather than resolving it. Owner: Legal.

The documentation gate, stepping up with value (D11)

The documents a transmission needs step up across value bands, computed at the PAN level on the aggregate of all folios in the claim. The bands govern the no-nominee legal-heir path most heavily, but the attestation step-up applies to a nominee claim too. The figures are confirmed against the AMFI Best Practices Guidelines, now consolidated into the 2026 master circulars.

PAN-level valueWhat the documentation requiresEffect
Up to ₹5 lakhSimplified documentation: the transmission request form, the death certificate, the claimant's KYC and bank proof, and a bank-manager attestation of the claimant's signature. For a no-nominee claim, relationship proof.The light path. Most retail claims sit here and complete in working days, not months.
More than ₹5 lakh, less than ₹10 lakhSignature attested by a Notary Public or a Judicial Magistrate; individual affidavits from each legal heir; and for a no-nominee claim, either a notarised registered will with an indemnity bond, or a notarised no-objection from all the other legal heirs.The middle band. Heavier attestation and heir coordination, but still short of court process.
More than ₹10 lakhA probated will, or a letter of administration or court decree in the case of intestate succession.The court-process band. The slowest and most expensive, and the strongest argument for nominating in the first place.

An indemnity bond and the minor-guardian attestation rules layer on top of these bands, and a minimum cooling-off period, commonly ten business days and longer at some fund houses, runs after the transmission is processed, during which no redemption is permitted on the folio. These are value-and-timing details on the gate, not changes to the spine, so they are bulleted in Section 4.

3.Failure taxonomy

Transmission breaks in four characteristic ways, and the dangerous ones are not the missing-document cases but the contested and the multi-folio cases, where the firm is tempted to do more than facilitate.

How it breaksCauseWhere it lands, and recovery
Incomplete or mismatched documentationA document missing for the value band, a name or relationship that does not match, or an attestation in the wrong form.The claim is held, not failed: the RTA cannot effect the transfer until the paperwork is in order. The firm guides the claimant to the exact correction; this is the most common and most preventable failure.
Claim contested between nominee and heirsA nominee and the legal heirs, or heirs among themselves, disagree on who is entitled.An escalation, not a flow. The platform does not adjudicate; it routes the dispute to legal resolution between the parties and holds the transmission until it is resolved or a court directs. This is the D12 boundary in practice.
Transmission across many folios and AMCsOne deceased investor held across several folios and several fund houses, so the claim is not one event.Per-folio progress, not one atomic transmission: the PAN-level threshold is computed in aggregate, but each folio transmits on its own RTA, at its own pace. The firm tracks the whole as a set of claims and surfaces partial progress honestly.
Claimant not KYC compliantThe nominee or heir who is to receive the units does not hold a transactable identity.Transmission completes only once the claimant is KYC compliant, so the claimant is routed through their own onboarding (J1) or status restoration (J2) first. The transferee must be able to transact in their own right before the units can land with them.

Red flag: the platform must not adjudicate, and must not let a partial multi-folio transmission look complete

Two errors do real harm here. The first is the platform straying into deciding a contested claim, which it has no standing to do and which can route a holding away from its rightful owner; a contest is always an escalation to legal resolution, never a platform decision. The second is presenting a multi-folio, multi-AMC transmission as a single event that is either done or not done, when in reality each folio progresses separately and a family may be waiting on the slowest RTA while others have completed. The honest design tracks each folio's claim distinctly and never shows a grieving family a green checkmark that is only partly true.

4.Special cases

Variants that change a value, add a document, or layer a persona without rerouting the three paths. Listed, never drawn.

  • Minor nominee or heir, operated by a guardian. The guardian operates the transmitted holding until the minor attains majority, when J2's minor-to-major reroute takes over. An indemnity is obtained from the guardian, and the guardian's signature is attested against the minor's bank account or the minor's joint account with the guardian. The minor does not act in their own right until majority.
  • NRI nominee or heir. Permitted, with consequences that arrive later, not at transmission: repatriability follows the account class the units land in, and capital-gains TDS applies on a later redemption by the non-resident, not on the transmission itself. The death certificate may need apostille or consularisation, and a claimant who lacks an Indian PAN, KYC, or bank account follows the specific non-resident claimant path the AMFI guidelines provide. The repatriation and TDS overlays sit with J9 and the Investor reference when the NRI later redeems.
  • All holders deceased on a joint folio. Survivorship is exhausted, so the folio falls to the nominee, or to the legal heirs where there is no nominee, by the same paths as a sole folio. The joint structure no longer helps once there is no survivor.
  • Encumbered units. A lien or pledge survives transmission: the units transmit to the new holder still encumbered, and the encumbrance must be released by its holder before those units can be redeemed. Transmission moves ownership, not the charge on it.
  • No nomination changes after intimation, the J4 interplay. The moment a death is intimated, J4's nomination flow is frozen on the folio, so a nomination cannot be added, changed, or removed during transmission. The new holder sets nomination afresh only after the units have transmitted to them.
  • Cooling-off and stop-marking. Folios where the deceased is the first holder are stop-marked on intimation, and a cooling-off period runs after the transmission is processed, during which no redemption is permitted. A fraud and verification control, with a formal communication sent to the deceased's registered address.
  • Transmission is not a taxable event. India levies no inheritance tax and a transmission is not a sale, so no capital-gains arises on the transfer itself. A SEBI reason code introduced in September 2025 ensures the transfer is reported to the tax authority as a transmission rather than wrongly assessed as a capital-gains transfer. The gain, and any TDS, arise only when the new holder later redeems.
  • Demat holdings. Where the deceased held in demat rather than SOA form, transmission runs through the depository on the account rather than folio by folio on each RTA, which is the lighter operational path the Folio reference notes; V1's SOA-first scope means the per-folio path is the common one.

None of this blocks the wave; J12 completes the journey layer with these two items routed to Legal and to Product and Operations.
