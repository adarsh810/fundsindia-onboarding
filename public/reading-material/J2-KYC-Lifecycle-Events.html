J2: KYC Lifecycle Events

What happens to a transactable identity after J1 created it: the events that downgrade it, the path that restores it, and the one that reroutes it entirely when a minor attains majority. The standing life of the KYC status the Order pre-flight reads on every transaction.

AudienceEngineering & Product

What this document is

The governing view of how a KYC identity changes after onboarding. J1 drew the front door, a new investor reaching a transactable Validated status. J2 owns what happens to that status afterwards, because KYC is not a one-time event. A Validated record can be knocked down by a registry flag, a lapsed Aadhaar linkage that turns the PAN inoperative, or an expiring document, and it has to be restored before the investor can transact freely again. Separately, a minor's record reaches a hard fork on the eighteenth birthday and reroutes entirely. J2 draws the canonical downgrade-and-restore loop and the minor-to-major reroute, maps the registry failure modes the Rails reference owns to what the investor can and cannot do in each, and surfaces the one genuine product call this journey forces: what a KYC downgrade should do to a standing plan. It draws on Investor for the status regime and the lifecycle state model, Rails for the registries that own the truth, and Order for the pre-flight gate that consumes the status.

What it is not

Not a reference and not a PRD. It does not redraw onboarding, which is J1, and it does not own the KYC status definitions, which are the Investor reference's. The persona set is the locked V1 four, resident individual, joint, minor, and NRI, so the undivided-family partition event is out of scope and noted only as deferred. The heavy residency-status change, resident to non-resident and back, is bulleted with its blast radius rather than drawn, because servicing it fully may be its own journey, which is the second decision J2 surfaces. D2, the requirement that a non-resident be Validated to transact, is applied as settled.

1.The spine

J2 has no single happy path, because it is event-driven: the journey is the set of transitions a KYC identity can take after it exists. Two of those transitions are common and structural enough to draw. The first is the downgrade-and-restore loop, the everyday case where a Validated record slips to On Hold or Registered and has to climb back. The second is attaining majority, the once-per-minor event that does not downgrade the record so much as replace the person behind it. Everything else, contact changes, dormancy, residency shifts, is a value change or a check on top of these, and is bulleted in Section 4.

The downgrade-and-restore loop

flowchart TD
  A([Validated identity, transacting normally]) --> B{A validation trigger fires}
  B -->|a registry flag, a lapsed PAN-Aadhaar linkage, or an expired document| C([On Hold, new investment and SIPs blocked, redemptions blocked in some cases])
  B -->|an attribute cannot be database-verified but mobile and email still hold| D([Registered, can continue at existing fund houses, blocked at a new one])
  C --> E[Investor re-submits KYC, Aadhaar as the preferred document, and corrects the failed attribute]
  D --> E
  E --> F[The KRA re-validates the record against the official databases]
  F -->|verified| G([Validated, full access restored])
  F -->|still deficient| C

The two downgrade landing states differ in severity. Registered is the softer one: the investor can keep transacting at fund houses where they already hold, but cannot open a position at a new one until the record is pushed up to Validated, which the registry framework is actively encouraging. On Hold is the hard one: it blocks all new investment and standing instructions, and in some cases blocks redemptions in existing folios too, which is the harsh edge, because it can trap an investor's own money. The restore path is the same from either: re-submit the KYC, preferably with Aadhaar so the record reaches full Validated status rather than only Registered, the KRA re-validates against the Income Tax and Aadhaar databases, and the status lifts. If the re-submission is still deficient it falls back to On Hold rather than completing.

Attaining majority, the minor-to-major reroute

flowchart TD
  A([Minor's folio, operated by the guardian]) --> B{The minor turns eighteen}
  B -->|majority reached| C([Account frozen for transactions, the minor-attaining-majority mid-transition state])
  C --> D[The erstwhile minor completes KYC in their own right, supplies their PAN and a fresh bank mandate]
  D --> E{KYC validated and the new mandate registered}
  E -->|not yet| C
  E -->|yes| F([Guardian authority ceases, clubbing with the parent stops, operation transfers to the new major])
  F --> G([Account reactivated, the major transacts in their own name])

This is the transition that genuinely reroutes rather than merely downgrades, because the person operating the account changes. On the eighteenth birthday the folio freezes for transactions and enters the minor-attaining-majority mid-transition state the Investor reference flags as routinely under-built. It stays frozen, not failing but waiting, until the erstwhile minor has completed their own KYC, supplied their own PAN, and registered a fresh bank mandate in their own name. Only then does guardian authority cease, the tax subject change identity so that income stops being clubbed with the parent, and operation transfer. A platform that treats this as an attribute flip rather than a freeze-and-rebuild will let a guardian keep operating an adult's account, or strand the account in a half-state where neither the guardian nor the new major can act.

Who acts at each step

StepActing party (by role)What the firm does
Detect the triggerThe KRA, the Income Tax Department, or the calendar.Reflects a status change the registry pushes, an inoperative PAN the Income Tax database reports, a document expiry, or a date of birth reaching eighteen. The firm does not own the truth; it reconciles to it.
Inform and gateThe firm.Tells the investor what changed and what it blocks, and applies the gate so the Order pre-flight refuses what the status forbids. The cheapest place to prevent a transaction the registry would reject.
Re-submit or rebuildThe investor.Re-submits KYC with the corrected attribute, or for majority completes a fresh KYC, PAN, and mandate. The firm captures and routes to the KRA; it does not validate.
Re-validate and restoreThe KRA.Re-checks against the official databases and returns the new status. The firm reflects it and lifts or maintains the gate accordingly.

Key principle: status is a function of attributes and current state, and J2 only sequences the transitions

The Investor reference models KYC, eligibility, and tax as functions of an investor's attributes and current lifecycle state rather than as fixed labels, and it owns the status ladder and the Part V transition table. J2 adds no new status and no new transition; it draws the order of operations around the two transitions that reroute a real flow, and it leans on the Order pre-flight to enforce whatever the current status permits. The discipline this protects is that a status is never edited directly to unblock an investor; it changes only when the registry that owns it says so.

2.Decision points

One genuine product call this journey forces and must surface as a new register row, drawn, because either answer reroutes what a standing plan does.

What a KYC downgrade does to a standing plan

This is the decision J2 exists to surface. The status ladder is clear that On Hold blocks new investment and SIPs, but a SIP that is already Active sits in an ambiguous place: is each instalment a new investment the gate must refuse, or is the plan a standing thing that should react to the downgrade as a whole. No external rule settles it; it is a product and engineering choice, and it carries investor harm either way.

flowchart TD
  A([A KYC downgrade hits an investor who has an Active SIP]) --> B{What happens to the standing plan}
  B -->|option one, recommended| C([Plan proactively Paused, the investor is told why, no instalments attempt while On Hold])
  B -->|option two| D([Plan stays Active, each instalment fails at the J6 pre-flight on the status gate])
  D --> E([Failed instalments count toward the D9 pause threshold, the plan pauses only after several])
  C --> F([On restore to Validated, the investor resumes the plan])
  E --> F

Open: plan behaviour on a KYC downgrade is a new product call (D46)

The recommended position is to pause the plan proactively the moment the identity goes On Hold, with a clear message that the downgrade, not the funding, is the cause, and to resume on restore. The alternative, letting each instalment fail at the J6 pre-flight, has two problems: it produces a run of debit-style failures for a reason that has nothing to do with the mandate, and it entangles the downgrade with D9, since those failures would count toward the consecutive-failure pause and the investor would be told their funding failed when it did not. The proactive pause is cleaner and kinder, but it needs Product and Engineering to settle how it interacts with D9, whether a downgrade-pause is a distinct plan state from a funding-pause, and what happens to a SWP, whose instalments have no debit to fail. This is a new register row, D46, routed to Product and Engineering.

3.Failure taxonomy

The Rails reference names five ways the identity rail breaks, each of which lands the investor in a different position. J2 maps them to what the investor can and cannot do while in each, because the harm is not uniform.

How it breaksCauseWhere it lands, and what the investor can do
Fetch failureA registry call fails transiently, so the current status cannot be read at the moment it is needed.A technical failure, not a status change. The transaction waits and retries; the firm must not infer a downgrade from a failed read, which would block an investor whose record is fine.
Validation pendingA re-submission has been sent to the KRA and not yet returned.The record sits in its prior state until the KRA responds. The investor is told it is in progress; nothing is upgraded optimistically before the registry confirms.
Status downgradeThe KRA moves the record to Registered or On Hold on a failed attribute.The downgrade-and-restore loop in Section 1. Registered limits the investor to existing fund houses; On Hold blocks new investment and standing plans and sometimes redemptions.
eKYC outageThe Aadhaar eKYC channel is unavailable during a re-submission.The restore stalls at submission, not at validation. The investor can complete through an alternative modality, or wait for the channel; the firm should not push the record to a lower status because a channel was down.
Inoperative PANThe PAN-Aadhaar linkage lapses, so the Income Tax database reports the PAN as inoperative.A pre-flight block, not a post-submission failure: the unlinked PAN drops the record to a blocked state that stops even redemptions. The investor re-links the PAN with Aadhaar, pays the prescribed fee, and the PAN becomes operative again within the statutory window, after which the record can be re-validated.

Red flag: a downgrade that blocks redemptions can trap an investor's own money

The harsh edge of this journey is that an On Hold status, or an inoperative PAN, can block not just new investment but redemptions in existing folios, which means an investor can be locked out of money that is already theirs. This is a compliance reality, not a platform choice, but the platform's duty is to make the cause and the remedy unmistakable, to never present it as the firm's decision, and to route the investor to the fastest restore path rather than leaving them to discover the block at the moment they try to withdraw. The worst version of this is silent: an investor who learns of the downgrade only when a redemption fails.

4.Special cases

Transitions and events that change a value or add a check without rerouting the two drawn flows. Listed, never drawn.

  • Residency-status change, resident to non-resident and back. The heaviest event J2 carries, and the reason it is bulleted rather than drawn: it is not one status change but several at once. It moves the KYC record, changes the bank account class from resident to NRE or NRO, re-tags the folio's repatriability, shifts tax residency and withholding, and pulls in FATCA and CRS reporting. The Investor reference flags the return leg, non-resident to resident, as routinely under-built, because teams build the departure and forget the homecoming. J2 records the blast radius and routes the full servicing flow to a candidate later journey; whether that flow is in V1 scope is the second decision below (D47).
  • Contact and bank-detail changes. Updating a mobile number, email, or bank account triggers a re-validation of the changed attribute, since mobile and email are validated attributes and the bank account underpins payouts and mandates. A value change with a verification step, not a status reroute, though a failed re-validation can itself cause a downgrade, which lands back in Section 1.
  • Dormancy marking and reactivation. A long-inactive record can be marked dormant and reactivated on the investor's return, a state on the record rather than a KYC downgrade. Reactivation may itself prompt a re-KYC if the dormancy outlasted a re-verification window.
  • Periodic re-KYC. KYC is time-bound and risk-based, broadly re-verified every two years for high-risk, eight for medium, and ten for low-risk clients under the banking regime, with the KRA layering its own re-validation triggers on top, the push from Registered to Validated being the live one. A re-KYC scheduler keyed to a risk classification belongs in the model, so these are anticipated events, not surprises.
  • Accreditation gain or loss. Meeting or ceasing to meet the accredited-investor thresholds, or a certificate expiring, toggles access to accredited-only treatment such as the SIF minimum-ticket exemption. A capability toggle on the record, not a KYC status move.
  • Undivided-family partition, out of V1 scope. The partition of an undivided family changes its PAN and tax status and apportions its holdings, but the undivided-family persona is deferred from V1, so this event is noted as deferred rather than drawn.
  • The tightening Validated bar. The registry framework moved during 2026 toward requiring Aadhaar and PAN authentication for full validation and toward pushing non-Aadhaar records up from Registered, so the practical bar for Validated has risen. The exact post-tightening KRA rule should be confirmed with Compliance and the KRA before build, since the grounding here is contemporaneous reporting rather than a single primary circular.
  • The non-resident branch. A non-resident must be Validated to transact, applied as settled per D2; the registry relaxation that once let an NRI transact on Registered status has lapsed, so the downgrade-and-restore loop applies to non-residents with no softer path.

Key principle: J2 sequences transitions it does not own, and adds two register rows rather than a reference gap

Every status and every transition J2 uses is the Investor reference's, and every registry and failure mode is the Rails reference's, so J2 flags no reference gap. What it adds is product surface the references could not settle: what a downgrade does to a standing plan (D46) and whether the residency-change servicing flow is in V1 scope and its own journey (D47). Both are genuine choices with investor-harm consequences, not facts a reference can carry, which is why they are register rows and not reference edits.

Open: what hardens J2

J2 is drawn against the Investor reference's settled status ladder and transition model, so its two flows are stable. Two product calls sit on top. D46 needs Product and Engineering to decide how a standing plan reacts to a downgrade and how that reconciles with the D9 pause, since the recommended proactive pause is cleaner but introduces a second kind of pause to model. D47 needs Product and Compliance to rule whether the heavy residency-change flow is in V1 and whether it earns its own journey, with the non-resident-to-resident return leg called out as the part teams forget. A third, lighter item is to confirm the tightened post-2026 KRA validation bar against the primary KRA and SEBI material before build, since it is grounded here in contemporaneous reporting. None of this blocks the wave.
