J4: Nomination Capture and Update

The standing life of a nomination on a live folio: viewing, adding, changing, or removing nominees and shares, or switching to a formal opt-out, with the consent the folio's ownership demands and the instruction registered with the RTA. The continuation of the creation-time gate J3 drew, governed now by the SEBI circular dated 29 May 2026.

Statusv0.1: Decision-surfacing draft
DateJune 2026
AudienceEngineering & Product

What this document is

The governing view of how a nomination lives and changes after the folio exists. J3 drew the creation-time gate, the moment a single-holder folio must nominate or formally opt out before it can come alive. J4 owns everything after that: the investor returning to view who is nominated, to add or remove a nominee, to re-weight the shares, or to flip between a nomination and a formal opt-out, on a folio that is already live. A nomination is not an order and has no money leg; it is an instruction captured against the folio, registered with the RTA, and reflected by the platform. J4 draws who may make that instruction, the consent a joint folio demands, the data the instruction must carry, and the one moment a nomination change must be refused outright. It draws on Folio for the nomination machinery, Investor for operating authority, and J3 for the creation gate it continues.

What it is not

Not a reference and not a PRD. It does not redraw folio creation, which is J3, and it does not execute transmission, which is J12. The boundary with J12 is the hard one: a nomination is the instruction that drives transmission on death, but J4 stops at the living investor's changes, and the moment a death is intimated the nomination freezes and the folio passes to J12. J4 builds to the operative nomination framework, now settled rather than in flight: the SEBI circular dated 29 May 2026, effective 1 September 2026, which the Folio reference carries at v0.2 and J3 at v0.3. The presentation rule the Folio reference flags as a red flag is carried here in full, because the place an investor edits a nomination is exactly where the platform is tempted to misdescribe what a nominee is.

1.The spine

A nomination update is a short flow with two heavy gates and a long shadow. The flow is simple: an investor changes who is nominated, the instruction registers with the RTA, the platform reflects it. The first gate is authority, only the investor may nominate, never a power-of-attorney holder, and a joint folio needs every holder to consent. The second gate is timing, a nomination cannot be changed once a death has been intimated on the folio, because at that point survivorship or transmission has taken over and the change would route units to the wrong person. The long shadow is that this instruction is the thing that decides, years later, who can reach the holding quickly when the investor dies, which is why the presentation of it matters as much as the mechanics.

flowchart TD
  A([Investor initiates a nomination action on a live folio, add, change, remove, or switch to opt-out]) --> B{Folio is live and carries no death intimation}
  B -->|death intimation present| X1([Blocked, the nomination is frozen and the folio routes to J12, see failure taxonomy])
  B -->|live folio| C{Authority and consent satisfied}
  C -->|not satisfied| X2([Blocked, only the investor may nominate, and a joint folio needs all-holder consent, see failure taxonomy])
  C -->|satisfied| D[Capture the change, name and relationship mandatory, up to three nominees, shares total the whole or split equally by default]
  D --> E[Register the instruction with the RTA, folio by folio in SOA form]
  E --> F([RTA acknowledges, platform reflects the updated nomination or opt-out])

The two gates are the whole story. Authority is checked first because a nomination made by the wrong party is void no matter how clean the data: the circular is explicit that only the investor nominates, and on a joint folio the consent of every holder is required to provide or change a nomination irrespective of the operation mode, so even an either-or-survivor folio cannot have its nominee changed by one holder acting alone. The timing gate is checked because a nomination change after a death intimation is not a correction, it is an attempt to redirect a holding that has already begun to pass, and it must be refused and routed to J12. Past both gates the capture is light, name and relationship are the only mandatory fields under the 29 May 2026 circular, and the registration is folio by folio because in SOA form the nominee attaches to the folio, not to the investor.

Who acts at each step

StepActing party (by role)What the firm does

Obtain consentThe investor, and on a joint folio every joint holder.For a joint folio, collects all-holder consent before the change can proceed. How consent is evidenced is a build choice; that it is required is not.

2.Decision points

One genuine fork that decides whether the change is even permitted, drawn, and one toggle the investor controls, drawn lightly. The count and field rules are no longer a fork, because the circular settled them.

The authority and consent fork

This is the gate that makes or breaks the change. It has two independent conditions, the acting party and, for a joint folio, the consent of the others, and both must pass.

flowchart TD
  P([A nomination change is requested on a live folio]) --> Q{Acting party}
  Q -->|power-of-attorney holder| M1([Refused, a power-of-attorney holder cannot nominate])
  Q -->|the investor| R{Folio ownership}
  R -->|single-holder| M2([Proceed, the investor's instruction alone suffices])
  R -->|jointly held| S{Consent of all joint holders obtained}
  S -->|no| M3([Held, the change cannot register until every holder consents])
  S -->|yes| M2

The investor-only rule comes from the Investor reference and the circular together: a nomination is a personal act, and a power of attorney that can place orders still cannot make or change a nomination. The all-holder-consent rule is the one the 29 May 2026 circular hardened, and it is the reason this is drawn rather than bulleted: it reroutes the path for a joint folio and it overrides the operation mode, so a folio that lets one holder transact alone still needs both holders to agree a nominee. How the firm evidences that consent, a co-sign, a second authentication, a dual approval, is PRD latitude; that it is mandatory is drawn here.

The nominate-versus-opt-out toggle

An investor can hold a nomination, or formally opt out, and can move between the two as often as they like. Each direction is a registrable instruction, not a silent UI state, because the opt-out is itself a declaration the RTA records.

flowchart TD
  A([Current nomination state on the folio]) --> B{Investor's choice}
  B -->|nominate or re-nominate| C([Capture up to three nominees and shares, register with the RTA])
  B -->|opt out| D([Capture the affirmed opt-out declaration, register with the RTA])
  C --> E([Reflected, the investor can change it any number of times later])
  D --> E

D10 is applied as settled, not surfaced as open

When J3 drew the creation gate, the nominee count and mandatory-field set were an open call, D10, because a SEBI consultation was in flight. That call is now closed. The circular dated 29 May 2026 set the maximum at three nominees, made name and relationship the only mandatory fields, and defaulted to an equal split with the odd lot to the first nominee. J4 therefore builds to three with no fork on the count, applying D10 as settled. The single residual is a Compliance confirmation of the provisions against the primary circular text before the capture form is finalised, which the Folio reference carries.

3.Failure taxonomy

A nomination update breaks in three ordinary ways and one that is a red flag. The ordinary failures return the investor to correct and retry; the red-flag failure must be refused and handed to another journey.

How it breaksCauseWhere it lands, and recovery
Wrong acting partyA power-of-attorney holder, or anyone other than the investor, attempts to make or change the nomination.Refused at the authority gate, before anything registers. Only the investor can proceed; the holder of a power of attorney cannot be substituted here.
Incomplete joint consentOn a joint folio, one or more holders have not consented to the change.Held, not failed: the instruction cannot register until every holder consents. It resumes when the missing consent arrives, and lapses if it does not.
RTA rejection on nominee dataMalformed or inconsistent data the RTA will not accept, shares that do not total the whole, more than three nominees, or a missing mandatory field.Returned for correction before registration completes. Validate at capture so this is caught early; the share split and the three-nominee ceiling are the common offenders.
Change attempted after a death intimationA nomination add, change, or removal requested once a death has been intimated on the folio.Refused and routed to J12. This is the red flag below: the holding has begun to pass, and a nomination change at this point would redirect it. Never allowed, never queued.

Red flag: no nomination change survives a death intimation

The most dangerous request J4 can receive is a nomination change after a holder's death has been intimated. On a single-holder folio the holding is already transmitting to the nominee or the heirs, and on a joint folio survivorship has already passed it to the survivor; a change at this moment is an attempt, innocent or not, to redirect a holding that is no longer the deceased's to redirect. The firm must hard-block any nomination instruction once a death intimation exists on the folio and route the folio to J12, rather than treat the change as a late edit. This is also why J4 and J12 share a clean seam: J4 owns the living investor's changes, J12 owns everything from the intimation onward.

4.Special cases

Variants that change a value or add a check without rerouting the update flow. Listed, never drawn.

  • Minor nominee. A minor can be nominated, with the date of birth captured so majority can be tracked. Under the 29 May 2026 circular the guardian details for a minor nominee are now optional rather than mandatory, a reduction from the earlier regime; the firm may still capture a guardian, but cannot block the nomination for its absence.
  • NRI nominee. A non-resident can be nominated. The repatriability and TDS consequences do not arise at nomination; they arise only on a later transmission and redemption, and sit with J12 and the repatriation path, not here. J4 captures the nominee; it does not pre-judge the remittance.
  • Opt-out reversal. An investor who opted out can later nominate, and a nominated investor can later opt out, any number of times. Each is a fresh registrable instruction; there is no penalty and no lock, and the reminder regime stops once a nomination or a fresh opt-out is on record.
  • The reminder regime. An investor who has neither nominated nor opted out is sent periodic reminders, by SMS and email and an in-app prompt, rather than having the folio frozen. The earlier freeze threat was dropped; J4's surfaces are where these reminders resolve into an action.
  • Per-folio capture in SOA form. A nominee attaches to the folio, so an investor with several folios at one AMC updates each folio separately, and a single intended change can mean several registrations. A demat account, by contrast, carries one nomination across everything in it. This is the operational cost of the SOA form, inherited from the Folio reference.
  • Equal apportionment and the odd lot. Where shares are left unspecified across multiple nominees, the holding is split equally, and any indivisible odd lot goes to the first nominee named. A default the capture applies, not a decision the investor must make.
  • Persona authority. A minor's folio is operated by the guardian, but the guardian operates it, they do not nominate on the minor's behalf in the investor sense; nomination on a minor's folio follows the framework the guardian administers until the minor attains majority, which is a J2 event. A joint folio's nomination follows the all-holder-consent rule regardless of who may transact.

Red flag: a nominee is a trustee, not an owner, and the edit screen is where this is misread

Carried from the Folio reference because J4 is where it bites. A registered nominee receives the units on death so the family can reach them quickly, but holds them as a caretaker for the legal heirs, whose claim under a will or succession law can prevail. The screen where an investor adds or weights nominees is the exact place a platform is tempted to call it estate planning, to imply that naming a nominee settles who inherits. It does not. J4 must present nomination as an access mechanism and never as a substitute for a will, and on a joint folio it must not imply that a nominee will receive ahead of the surviving holder, because survivorship comes first.

Key principle: J4 sequences an attachment, it does not own a lifecycle, and it adds no row

A nomination has no lifecycle of its own; it is a value on the folio that the investor can change while the folio is live. J4's work is the order of operations around that value, the authority to change it, the consent a joint folio demands, the data it must carry, and the one moment it must be frozen. Because the Folio reference now carries the operative framework in full at v0.2, J4 invents nothing and flags no gap. It applies D10 as settled and brushes D12, adding no register row.

Open: what hardens J4

J4 is drawn against a settled framework, so its spine and gates are stable. Two things sit just outside it. The first is the residual on D10: Compliance should confirm the 29 May 2026 circular's provisions, the three-nominee maximum, the mandatory fields, and the joint-consent and minor-guardian changes, against the primary circular text before the capture form is built, since the grounding here is a reading of the circular rather than the gazette. The second is D12: the precedence judgment J4 routes into is the contested legal call J12 carries, with the Legal opinion sought from the week of 15 June; J4 does not need it resolved to draw the freeze-and-route boundary, but J12 will. Neither blocks the wave. J4 adds no register row.
