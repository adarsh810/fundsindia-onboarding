J6: The Order Lifecycle Spine

The single-order lifecycle that every transaction journey instantiates: how one order moves from placed to settled, where the path forks on direction, and the four ways it breaks. The most reused flow in the platform, drawn rail-agnostic so it can govern before the rail is finally chosen.

Statusv0.1: Decision-surfacing draft
DateJune 2026
AudienceEngineering & Product
TypeJourney document (not a reference, not a PRD)

What this document is

The governing view of one order's end-to-end lifecycle: the canonical happy path from the moment an investor's intent becomes a valid order to the moment money and units have finished moving and the books reconcile, the single fork that genuinely reroutes that path, and the four ways it terminates short of success. It is the shared sub-journey that J7, J8, J9, J10, and J11 each instantiate, which is why it is drafted first and why hardening it pays off across every transaction flow. It draws on the Order reference for every state and transition, on Rails for which party acts at each leg, and references Investor, Folio, and Instrument for the pre-flight gates without restating them.

What it is not

Not a reference: it invents no entity and no state, it sequences the ones the Order reference owns. Not a PRD: it specifies no screens and no build tasks. And it is deliberately one order only. The standing-instruction layer where a plan spawns child orders belongs to J8, the two-leg sell-then-buy linkage belongs to J10, and the onboard-to-first-order thread belongs to J7. This is also a decision-surfacing first pass, per the layer method: it draws the skeleton to expose the decisions the drawing forces, routes those to the register, and hardens into a governing contract once they close. The spine itself is rail-agnostic, so it is governing on the mechanics today even while the rail decision is still settling.

1.The spine

One order moves through five states on the happy path and exits to one of two terminal failure states. The states are the Order reference's; the journey is the sequence between them. Naming each state explicitly, rather than inferring it from a timestamp, is what lets the platform answer "where is my money" correctly at any moment, because a single order can sit in flight across several days.

flowchart TD
  A([Placed]) -->|pre-flight pass| B[RoutedToRail]
  A -->|pre-flight fail| F([Failed])
  B -->|rail accepts and time-stamps| C[Acknowledged]
  B -->|money leg fails or rail rejects| F
  C -->|NAV struck and units or value allotted| D[Allotted]
  C -->|post-acknowledgement rejection| F
  D -->|settlement reconciles against RTA feed| S([Settled])
  D -->|dishonour or error after allotment| R([Reversed])

That is the whole governing altitude in one picture. The direction fork attaches as the decision point in Section 2, the exit causes are enumerated in the failure taxonomy in Section 3, and rarities such as a partial allotment are bulleted in Section 4. The diagram never tries to hold all of it.

Who acts at each leg

The spine is drawn rail-agnostic, but each transition is an action by a specific party, named here by role rather than by vendor, because the firm originates and tracks but never executes the allotment or holds the money. This is the level at which J6 draws on the Rails reference.

TransitionActing party (by role)What the firm does
Intent to PlacedThe firm.Constructs and validates the order: authority, eligibility, KYC status, suitability, an open cut-off window, and the direction-specific gate. Nothing has left the investor.
Placed to RoutedToRailThe order-routing rail (middleware or direct), and the payment rail for a purchase.Hands the order to the rail and, for a purchase, initiates the money leg on a non-pooled basis. The firm is now waiting, not acting.
RoutedToRail to AcknowledgedThe RTA (or the platform on its behalf).The order is registered and time-stamped externally. The applicable NAV becomes determinable from the cut-off and, for a purchase, the realisation of funds.
Acknowledged to AllottedThe AMC strikes the NAV; the RTA allots units or computes the redemption value.The firm consumes the struck NAV and reflects the allotment. It does not price or allot.
Allotted to SettledThe payment rail and, on the exchange path, the clearing corporation, between the investor's bank and the AMC; the RTA register is the truth.Reconciles its own projection against the RTA feed. Money never passes through the firm.

Key principle: the spine is rail-agnostic by design

The states and transitions above are identical whether the order routes through a transaction-infrastructure middleware today or a direct exchange adapter later. The rail changes who sits behind each leg and how a reversal is mechanically unwound, not the shape of the lifecycle. This is what lets J6 be governing on the mechanics before the rail decision (D1) closes, and it is the reason the firm keeps its own order adapter behind whichever rail a middleware abstracts, so the rail can be swapped without its model leaking inside.

2.Decision points

The spine has one fork that genuinely reroutes the path, and one gate cluster that either lets the order continue or sends it to Failed. Everything else is a check on a step already drawn, which belongs in the failure taxonomy or the special cases, not in the diagram.

The direction fork: money in versus money out

Direction is the one decision that changes the steps rather than just a value. A purchase depends on a payment that must succeed and realise before anything is priced, so it carries a funding gate and the funds-realisation rule. A redemption depends on units being available and unencumbered, carries no realisation dependency because no incoming money has to clear, and adds a payout leg measured in working days after the applicable NAV.

flowchart TD
  P([Placed]) --> DIR{Money in or money out}
  DIR -->|money in, purchase| B1[Funding instruction must exist
pre-flight adds a funding check]
  DIR -->|money out, redemption| S1[Units must be unencumbered
pre-flight adds a units check]
  B1 --> B2[RoutedToRail
payment leg initiated]
  S1 --> S2[RoutedToRail
redemption registered, no money leg]
  B2 --> B3[Acknowledged
NAV determinable on funds realisation]
  S2 --> S3[Acknowledged
same-day NAV if before cut-off]
  B3 --> B4[Allotted
units credited to the folio]
  S3 --> S4[Allotted
payout value computed]
  B4 --> B5([Settled
units on register, payment realised])
  S4 --> S5([Settled
proceeds in registered bank, T+n days])

Special attention: the price is not set at the click

On the money-in branch the applicable NAV cannot be known at Placed. Under forward pricing it is struck later that day, and under the funds-realisation rule it is the NAV of the day the funds actually reach the AMC, for every ticket size. A purchase placed before cut-off but funded a day later takes the later day's NAV. So the rail's speed is a pricing input, not just a convenience, and the platform must not promise a price it cannot honour. The money-out branch is simpler: a redemption registered before the cut-off takes the same day's NAV, and the complication there is the payout timing, not the price.

The pre-flight gate

Every order passes a single pre-flight gate before it leaves the firm. It is drawn as one gate, not as a row of separate forks, because every check that fails sends the order to the same place, Failed, with no money having moved. What matters at this altitude is not the branching but the catalogue of checks and, crucially, which the firm can see at Placed and which it cannot. The firm validates against its own reflected projection of external state, so a check is only as good as the freshness of that reflection, and some conditions are simply invisible until the RTA or AMC sees the order.

Pre-flight checkWhat it reads, and which reference owns itCaught at Placed?

The last row is the reason the Acknowledged to Failed rejection edge in Section 3 is real and not redundant with pre-flight. The firm catches what its reflected folio and KYC state can see; the rest surfaces only once the order is registered.

3.Failure taxonomy

The exception paths are where an order platform is judged, because the money is at risk only when something breaks part-way. There are four canonical exits, each mapped to a terminal state the Order reference owns. The decisive line between them is allotment: before it, unwinding is returning any money that moved, so the order Failed; at or after it, units exist or a payout was computed, so backing it out is a compensating action and the order is Reversed.

ExitFromToCanonical causes and handling
Pre-flight failurePlacedFailedA check in Section 2 fails: KYC On-Hold or blocked, an authority gap, a closed cut-off, a scheme closed to purchase, an unfunded mandate, or encumbered, locked-in, or insufficient units. No money has moved. The cheapest failure and the highest-leverage place to catch one.
Money-leg or rail failureRoutedToRailFailedA purchase payment fails for insufficient funds, an inactive or exceeded mandate, or a bank decline; or the rail rejects the order on receipt. Any funds that moved are returned. For a SIP instalment this is an event on the instalment, not the plan, which is J8's concern.
Post-acknowledgement rejectionAcknowledgedFailedThe AMC or RTA rejects a registered order: a compliance flag, a frozen or under-transmission folio, or a scheme-level restriction. These are largely the conditions pre-flight could not see. If units were already provisionally allotted, the exit is to Reversed instead.
Dishonour or error after allotmentAllottedReversedThe costly edge: a purchase whose payment is later dishonoured after units were allotted on a provisional basis, or an allotment found to be erroneous. The allotment is rescinded and the register corrected by a compensating entry, never a quiet deletion. The exact unwind is rail-specific.

Red flag: the dangerous window is between provisional allotment and confirmed realisation

On a purchase, the most exposed moment in the whole lifecycle is the gap where units have been allotted on a provisional basis but the payment is not yet confirmed realised. The investor may appear to hold something the money has not yet paid for. A platform that shows a confirmed holding in that window invites a Reversed it then has to explain. The conservative design treats a purchase as truly final only when allotment and realisation agree, which is precisely the product decision Section 6 surfaces.

4.Special cases

Rare, within-path conditions that change a value or add a check but never reroute the spine. Listed, never drawn.

  • Partial allotment. A purchase funded for less than its full value, or a redemption only partly serviceable, allots what is fundable or available and fails or does not generate the remainder. It changes a quantity on a step already drawn, not the path.
  • Settled-state reversal. The rare Settled to Reversed case, reserved for genuine error correction or a regulatory unwind, always effected as a compensating entry rather than a rewrite of history. Heavy enough to name, rare enough not to draw.
  • The persona payout overlay on the money-out leg. A resident redemption carries no TDS and is self-assessed; an NRI or OCI redemption is withheld at source on the same event, with a certificate issued, and the proceeds route by the folio's repatriability to an NRE or NRO account. This adds a step and changes the net value on the payout, it does not reroute the spine. Rate detail stays in the Investor reference.
  • Stale reflected state. Because pre-flight reads the firm's reflected projection of KYC, folio, and encumbrance state, a stale reflection can pass an order that the RTA then rejects after acknowledgement. A within-path caveat on the pre-flight gate, surfaced as a decision in Section 6.
  • SIF interval-window gating. For an interval-strategy SIF, a redemption placed outside the defined window does not run; it queues to the next window, making the Placed to RoutedToRail transition conditional on the window being open. A gate on the money-out leg for one instrument profile, confirmed per strategy from the offer document.
  • SIF large-ticket first purchase. The first SIF purchase clears the per-PAN aggregate minimum, which exceeds the UPI capital-markets ceiling and forces a high-value rail. A rail-and-value change on the buy leg, not a new path.
  • Instantiation by the transaction family. A SIP instalment, an SWP instalment, and each leg of a switch or STP each instantiate this one spine independently. The standing-instruction lifecycle and the two-leg linkage that compose them are out of J6 and live in J8, J10, and J11.

5.Entities and states exercised

The references J6 draws on, and the one place its drawing implies a refinement a reference does not yet carry. Per the precedence rule, J6 invents nothing: where it needs something the ontology lacks, it flags it for the reference to fix.

ReferenceWhat J6 exercises
OrderThe whole spine: the five happy states, the Failed and Reversed terminals, and every transition between them. J6 sequences these; it adds no state.
InvestorThe KYC status regime as the pre-flight KYC gate; the operating-authority models as the authority gate; the persona payout overlay (resident no TDS, NRI and OCI TDS at source) on the money-out leg.
FolioEncumbrance through lien and pledge as the unencumbered-units gate; the operation mode feeding the authority gate; the folio lifecycle states (active, frozen, under-transmission, dormant) that split pre-flight-catchable failures from post-acknowledgement rejections; repatriability routing the payout.
InstrumentForward pricing, the cut-off, the funds-realisation rule, and exit load on the buy and sell gates; the SIF interval-window and large-ticket modulations as special cases.
RailsThe acting party by role at each leg; the RTA as the central record-keeping dependency; the clearing corporation or payment rail as the non-pooled money path between investor bank and AMC.

Open: a candidate refinement to propagate to the Order reference

J6's money-in branch leans on a distinction the Order reference currently holds as a property rather than a state: Allotted on a purchase can be provisional, before realisation is confirmed, or final, once allotment and realisation agree. Whether that warrants an explicit provisional-versus-confirmed sub-state on the buy side, or stays a property of Allotted, is a question for the Order reference to settle, not for J6 to invent. It is tied directly to the holding-visibility decision below, and is routed to the Order reference per the precedence rule.

Key principle: the build-scope was settled upstream

J6 adds no new build-scope. The Order reference already classified it: order construction with pre-flight validation, the lifecycle state machine including the failure and reversal edges, payment-leg initiation within the non-pooling rule, cut-off and realisation handling, attribution, and reconciliation against the RTA feed are the firm's to own; NAV striking, allotment, and settlement are referential; reversal execution and grievance routing are facilitate. The firm builds the verb; the AMC and RTA execute and record it.
