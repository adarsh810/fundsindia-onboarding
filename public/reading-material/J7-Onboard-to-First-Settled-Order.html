J7: Onboard to First Settled Order

The marquee end-to-end thread: a brand-new investor from identity through a first lumpsum purchase to a settled holding. The day-one north star that touches every entity, drawn as a composition that instantiates J6 and references the front of J1 and the whole of J3 rather than re-deriving them.

What this document is

The governing end-to-end view of a new investor reaching their first settled holding. It runs three segments in sequence and draws the seams between them: identity, capture and full KYC to a transactable, Validated record; container, the folio, brought into existence by the first purchase rather than presupposed; and the order, a lumpsum instance of the J6 spine through to a settled holding. It is the journey the team builds first, which is why it earns an early draft, and it is the one place where the whole suite is exercised at once.

What it is not

Not a reference and not a PRD. It does not re-derive the KYC machinery, the folio model, or the order lifecycle: it instantiates J6 for the order segment and references the front of J1 and the whole of J3 for the other two, drawing the handoffs rather than restating the internals. The marquee is the resident-individual baseline; the NRI, OCI, Minor, and entity branches are J1's depth, not drawn here. This is a decision-surfacing first pass: the seams it draws force two sequencing decisions it routes to the register, and it hardens once those and the no-pooling design under D1 close. The demat-versus-SOA fork (D3) is already settled as SOA-only, so the container segment has a default rather than a branch.

1.The spine

A new resident investor moves through three segments to a first settled holding. Identity comes first, because nothing routes until the record is transactable. The container and the order are fused on the marquee path: the first purchase is what brings the folio into existence, since the RTA issues a folio number the first time an investor transacts with a fund house. So the happy path is not three separate flows chained together, it is one thread with a deliberate fusion at the container seam.

flowchart TD
  A([New investor intent]) --> B[Capture identity, bank, FATCA self-certification]
  B --> C[Full KYC via video or biometric, Aadhaar-based]
  C --> D{KYC status resolved}
  D -->|Validated| E([Transactable identity])
  D -->|On-Hold or Rejected| X1([Onboarding halts, remediate and retry])
  E --> F[Place first lumpsum, flagged as a new folio]
  F --> G[Order spine runs, Placed through Allotted]
  G --> H[RTA issues the folio number at first allotment]
  H --> I([First holding Settled in the new folio])
  G -->|order fails or reverses| X2([No holding, see the J6 taxonomy])

The order box is J6 in full, not a simplification of it. Drawing it as one node here is the altitude holding: J7 owns the thread and the seams, J6 owns the states inside that box. The two failure exits shown are the two that end the journey rather than a sub-step, a KYC that never reaches Validated and an order that fails or reverses; the finer order-level failures live in the J6 taxonomy and are referenced in Section 3.

Special attention: the folio is created by the order, not before it

The intuitive model, create the account, then transact into it, is not how mutual fund folios work. For a net-new investor at a new fund house, the folio number is issued by the RTA at the first allotment, so on the marquee path the container segment is fused into the order: the first lumpsum carries a new-folio instruction and the folio comes back with the allotment. Drawing folio creation as a mandatory discrete pre-step would model a sequence the rail does not require, and would miss that a first order and a folio creation are usually one event. Whether to keep the fusion or run folio creation standalone is the central seam, surfaced in Section 2.

2.Decision points

J7 has one genuine structural seam and one status fork. The seam is how the front of the journey is sequenced, which reroutes the early steps and is therefore drawn. The fork is what the KYC status permits, which gates whether the order may proceed at all.

The sequencing seam

Three sequencings are viable, and the choice changes the early path rather than a value, so it is a decision point and not a special case. The marquee is KYC-first with a fused folio. The other two are real alternatives the business may prefer.

flowchart TD
  K[KYC in progress] --> Q{Sequencing seam}
  Q -->|KYC-first then order| M1[Marquee path, reach Validated then order
folio created at allotment]
  Q -->|order held pending KYC| M2[Capture the order, hold before Placed
route only once Validated]
  Q -->|discrete folio first| M3[Create the folio standalone
then place the order into it]

The marquee, KYC-first then order, is the cleanest: the record reaches Validated, the first lumpsum is placed, and the folio is created at allotment. The order-held-pending-KYC variant lets an eager investor express the purchase while KYC is still resolving, holding the order before it is ever Placed and routing it only once the status clears; it improves conversion but, as Section 5 notes, it implies a state earlier than Placed that the Order reference does not yet carry. The discrete-folio-first variant runs J3 standalone before any order, which is the path when a folio is opened deliberately ahead of funding. All three are drawn here because the choice is the business's to make, and it is written back as a decision.

The KYC status fork

What the resolved KYC status permits is the gate on the whole journey, and for a net-new investor at a new fund house it is sharper than it first appears.

Resolved statusWhat it permits for this journeyJ7 outcome
ValidatedUnrestricted investment with any fund house. Earned by an Aadhaar-based validation against the official databases.Proceeds to the order segment.
RegisteredInvestment only with fund houses where a relationship already exists. Typically the result of a non-Aadhaar OVD that could not be database-validated.Insufficient for a new fund house. The journey must drive the record to Validated before the first order can route.
On-Hold or RejectedBlocks new investment entirely. Caused by an inoperative PAN, an unverified mobile or email, or a non-OVD document.Onboarding halts; the defect is remediated and the status re-resolved.

Red flag: Registered is not good enough for the first order

For the mass-affluent resident this journey targets, only Validated clears the first purchase, because Registered permits transacting only where a relationship already exists and J7 is by definition a new relationship. In practice that means the identity segment must use an Aadhaar-based full-KYC modality, video IPV or biometric, rather than a non-Aadhaar OVD that would settle at Registered and silently block the order at pre-flight. Designing the onboarding around a non-Aadhaar OVD would produce a record that looks complete and still cannot transact.

3.Failure taxonomy

A composed journey fails differently depending on which segment breaks, and how it recovers differs with it. This is the new shape J7 carries over a single-entity journey: three failure surfaces, not one. The order segment does not re-enumerate its breaks here, it inherits the J6 taxonomy wholesale.

SegmentHow it breaksWhere it lands, and recovery
IdentityKYC resolves to On-Hold or Rejected: an inoperative PAN, an unverified mobile or email, a non-OVD document; or it settles at Registered when only Aadhaar-based validation would clear a new fund house.The investor never reaches a transactable identity. Remediate the specific defect, link PAN and Aadhaar or redo on an Aadhaar OVD, and re-resolve. No folio and no order exist yet, so nothing has to be unwound.
ContainerFolio creation is rejected by the RTA for a data or name-PAN mismatch. On the fused path this surfaces as a not-in-good-order rejection of the first order at Acknowledged.Correct the folio data and re-place. Distinguishing this from a payment failure is the subtlety below, because both present as a rejected first order.
OrderThe full J6 taxonomy: pre-flight failure, money-leg or rail failure, post-acknowledgement rejection, and dishonour or error after allotment.As J6. A pre-flight or rail failure ends in Failed with no holding; a dishonour after a provisional allotment ends in Reversed.

Special attention: a rejected first order is ambiguous on the fused path

Because the marquee fuses folio creation into the first order, a container failure and an order failure can look identical at the surface, both are a first order that did not go through. The journey must tell apart "the folio data was not in good order" from "the payment failed" to route the investor to the right fix, since one is a data correction and the other is a funding retry. A flow that treats every failed first order the same will send half its new investors down the wrong recovery path.

4.Special cases

Rare or within-path conditions that change a value, add a check, or shorten a segment without rerouting the thread. Listed, never drawn.

  • Existing-KYC investor. A CKYC or KRA fetch that returns Validated collapses the identity segment to a fetch-and-confirm, skipping fresh KYC. A within-path shortcut, common for investors already KYC'd elsewhere, not a new path.
  • Fetched Registered status. A fetch that returns Registered adds an incremental Aadhaar-validation step before the order can route, since Registered does not clear a new fund house. An added check, not a reroute.
  • Folio reuse. If the investor already holds a folio with this AMC in the same holding mode and repatriability, the first purchase lands in the existing folio rather than creating one, and the container segment is a no-op. The marquee assumes net-new; this is the not-net-new variant, and its prevalence depends on the migration-scope question in Section 6.
  • Nomination at creation. Nomination is captured at folio creation as a nominate-or-opt-out step, so the container segment carries it; the depth of nomination capture and update is J4's. A step within the segment, not a fork.
  • Funding rail by ticket. A small first lumpsum rides UPI within the capital-markets ceiling; a larger one moves to netbanking, NEFT, or RTGS above it. A funding-rail and value change on the order leg, inherited from J6, not a new path.
  • The order segment is J6. Every order-level special case, partial allotment, the provisional-allotment visibility window, a settled-state reversal, applies and is not restated here. J7 inherits them by instantiating J6.

Open: two seam states to confirm with the owning references

First, the handoff from a Validated identity to a freshly created folio crosses two references: the Investor reference's transactable-identity state and the Folio reference's Created to Active transition. On the fused path these happen together, and whether that seam is modelled cleanly across the two references, or leaves a gap, is for Investor and Folio to confirm. Second, and sharper: if the business chooses the order-held-pending-KYC sequencing, an order must exist in a captured state before Placed, which the Order reference does not carry, since Placed already assumes pre-flight, including KYC, has passed. That option would force a pre-Placed draft state into the Order reference. Both are routed to the owning references per the precedence rule, not invented in this journey.
