J8: Systematic Investment Plan

How a SIP runs: a standing instruction, backed by a J5 mandate, that emits a child order on each scheduled date, each running the J6 order spine. The plan and the instalment keep separate lifecycles, so a single failed debit is an instalment event, never a plan stop.

AudienceEngineering & Product

What this document is

The governing view of how a Systematic Investment Plan runs. A SIP is not an order. It is a long-lived standing instruction with its own lifecycle that, on each scheduled date, debits a registered mandate and emits a child order that runs the full J6 spine on its own. J8 draws that plan lifecycle, Registered through Active to Completed or Cancelled, the per-instalment cycle with its pre-debit notification, and the rule that holds the whole design together: the plan and the instalment fail, pause, and terminate on separate clocks. It draws on Order for the plan and transaction family, on J5 for the mandate, and on J6 for the per-instalment spine.

What it is not

Not a reference and not a PRD. It does not redraw the per-instalment order, which is J6 instantiated each cycle, and it does not redraw mandate registration, which is J5. It is the money-in systematic plan specifically; the money-out plans, the SWP in J11 and the STP sell leg and switch in J10, reuse this plan-lifecycle pattern but need no mandate, so they are out of scope here. Per the settled D4 the mandate and its ceiling are taken as given. This is a decision-surfacing pass, and it centres the one genuinely open call it forces, the pause-on-failure policy (D9), which is a Product decision, not a default any source fixes.

1.The spine

A SIP has two altitudes, and keeping them apart is the whole craft of the journey. There is the plan, a long-lived instruction that is registered once and then lives until its tenure ends or the investor stops it. And there is the instalment, a fresh child order minted on each scheduled date that prices, allots, and settles on its own. The first diagram draws the plan lifecycle. The second draws what one scheduled date does. Neither redraws the J6 order spine, which each instalment runs in full.

The plan lifecycle

flowchart TD
  A([New SIP intent, scheme and amount and schedule]) --> B{A live mandate backs the plan}
  B -->|yes, from J5| C[Register the plan, schedule and amount and mandate and tenure]
  B -->|no live mandate| X1([Blocked, register a mandate first via J5])
  C --> D([Active, instalments generate on schedule])
  D -->|on each scheduled date| E[Emit a child order that runs the J6 spine]
  E -->|outcome recorded, plan unchanged by one result| D
  D -->|tenure or instalment count reached| G([Completed])

The plan is Registered once its schedule, amount, mandate link, and tenure are set, and it turns Active when instalments begin generating. From Active it loops, emitting an instalment each scheduled date and returning to Active regardless of how any single instalment turned out, until it reaches Completed at the end of its tenure or instalment count. Two states sit off this happy path and are reached deliberately: Paused, drawn in Section 2 as the failure-threshold fork and bulleted in Section 4 as the investor-initiated suspend, and Cancelled, the investor stopping the plan before its natural end. Cancelling stops future instalments only; it never reaches back to unwind instalments that already settled.

What one scheduled date does

flowchart TD
  A([Scheduled date reached]) --> B[Pre-debit notification to the investor, at least 24 hours ahead]
  B --> C{Investor skips this instalment}
  C -->|yes, via the notification| K([Skipped, plan and mandate untouched])
  C -->|no| D[Debit the mandate within its ceiling]
  D --> E[Emit a child order that runs the J6 spine, Placed through Settled]
  E --> F{Instalment outcome}
  F -->|settled| G([Instalment Settled, plan continues])
  F -->|debit or order fails| H([Instalment Failed, plan continues])

Every instalment opens with the mandatory pre-debit notification the e-mandate framework requires, at least twenty-four hours ahead, carrying the amount, the date, and an opt-out. That opt-out is the clean skip path: an investor can drop a single instalment without touching the plan or the mandate. If the instalment proceeds, the mandate is debited within its ceiling and a child order is emitted to run the J6 spine. Both outcomes, a settled instalment and a failed one, return the plan to Active. That symmetry is the point: the plan does not flinch at one result either way.

Who acts at each step

StepActing party (by role)What the firm does
Register the planThe firm.Captures the schedule, the amount, the mandate link, and the tenure. The plan is the firm's own entity; nothing is debited yet.
Pre-debit notificationThe firm, as the collecting party.Notifies the investor at least twenty-four hours before each debit, with an opt-out. A hard requirement under the e-mandate framework, not a courtesy.
Debit and emit the instalmentNPCI or the bank debits; the AMC and RTA price, allot, and settle.The firm emits the child order, initiates the debit, and tracks it through the J6 spine. Money never rests with the firm.
Record the outcomeThe firm.Reconciles each instalment against the RTA feed and records its result on the plan. The plan does not change state on a single instalment outcome.

Key principle: two entities, two clocks

The plan and the instalment are separate entities with separate lifecycles, and almost every systematic-plan defect traces back to collapsing them. The plan carries the schedule, the mandate, and the start and end conditions; each instalment carries a price, an allotment, and a settlement. A failed instalment must not fail the plan, and a cancelled plan must not unwind instalments that already settled. J8 owns the plan clock and the discipline of not letting one instalment move it.

2.Decision points

Two genuine forks J8 owns. The first is the open call this journey exists to surface; the second exercises a row J5 added and confirms it holds when a real flow leans on it.

The pause-on-failure threshold (D9)

This is the decision J8 centres, and it is genuinely open: no external rule fixes how many failures should pause a plan, so it is a Product policy. A single failed debit marks that instalment Failed and leaves the plan Active; the plan pauses only once a threshold of consecutive failures is crossed.

flowchart TD
  P([Instalment fails to debit or the order is rejected]) --> Q[Mark this instalment Failed, plan stays Active]
  Q --> R{Consecutive-failure threshold reached}
  R -->|no| S([Plan continues, next instalment generates on schedule])
  R -->|yes| T([Plan Paused, investor prompted to restore funding])

Open: the threshold and the retry behaviour are a Product call (D9)

Two parts need Product to settle. The threshold, how many consecutive failures pause the plan, is recommended at three consecutive failures, but that figure is a proposed default this journey puts forward, not a fact from any source. The retry behaviour, whether and how a failed instalment is re-attempted, must be defined alongside it and must never re-attempt in a way that double-invests. The recommended position is to mark the instalment Failed, not retry the same instalment within its cycle, and pause the plan after three consecutive failures with a prompt to restore funding. Owner: Product.

Reuse a mandate or register a new one (D44)

When a plan is registered, it needs a live mandate to debit. It can attach to an existing mandate already registered on the funding account, or trigger a fresh registration via J5. This fork is where the mandate-to-plan cardinality from D44 is exercised.

flowchart TD
  P([New SIP, funding account chosen]) --> Q{A live mandate on this account covers the instalment within its ceiling}
  Q -->|yes| M1([Attach the existing mandate, one mandate backing several plans])
  Q -->|no, or the amount exceeds the ceiling| M2([Register a new mandate via J5, then attach])

The recommended position from D44 holds when drawn here: a single mandate registered on a bank account, within its per-debit ceiling, can back more than one SIP, so the plan attaches an existing mandate where one covers it and registers a new one only when none does or the instalment exceeds the ceiling. The ceiling logic is D4's, already settled: keep the instalment inside the ₹1 lakh enhanced no-authentication band so the common case needs no per-cycle factor.

3.Failure taxonomy

The ways a SIP breaks separate cleanly into instalment-level events, which the plan absorbs, and plan-level conditions, which change the plan's state. The dividing rule is the two-clocks principle: a single instalment outcome never moves the plan by itself.

How it breaksCauseWhere it lands, and recovery
Single instalment debit failureInsufficient funds, or a mandate momentarily not debitable, on one scheduled date.That instalment lands Failed; the plan stays Active and may count the failure toward the pause threshold (D9). Recovers on the next cycle, or sooner if the investor restores funding.
Repeated failures past the thresholdConsecutive failed debits crossing the D9 threshold.The plan moves to Paused. Recovers when the investor restores funding and resumes; settled instalments are untouched.
Mandate cancelled or expired under a live planThe backing mandate reaches its own terminal while the plan is still Active.The plan can no longer debit. It pauses pending a fresh mandate via J5; this is the mandate clock and the plan clock meeting, and the plan does not silently die.
Post-debit instalment failureA debit succeeds but the child order fails or reverses downstream, on the J6 Failed or Reversed edges.The instalment lands Failed or Reversed in J6, which owns the money-return; the plan continues. The plan must not re-emit a replacement instalment that double-invests.
Insufficient units on a money-out planOut of scope here, noted for the pattern: an SWP or STP sell leg with too few unencumbered units.The instalment simply does not generate; no mandate is involved. Owned by J11 and J10, which reuse this plan-lifecycle shape.

Red flag: a failed instalment is not a failed plan, and a retry must not double-invest

These are the two errors that do real harm. Treating one bounced debit as a reason to stop the plan strands an investor's long-term instruction over a transient funding gap. Re-attempting a failed instalment carelessly, or emitting a make-up instalment on top of one that actually went through, double-invests the investor's money. The plan absorbs a single failure, counts it toward a threshold, and never retries in a way that can invest twice. This rule is the reason the plan and the instalment are modelled as separate entities in the first place.

4.Special cases

Variants that change a value or add a check without rerouting the plan lifecycle. Listed, never drawn.

  • Step-up SIP. The instalment amount rises on a schedule. As long as each stepped amount stays within the mandate's per-debit ceiling, no re-registration is needed, which is exactly why the mandate is registered with headroom above the starting instalment (D4 and J5). A step above the ceiling needs a fresh mandate.
  • Investor-initiated pause and resume. Distinct from the failure-threshold pause: the investor suspends the plan deliberately and resumes it later. Same Paused state, different trigger, no instalments generate while paused.
  • Skip a single instalment. Through the pre-debit notification's opt-out, the investor drops one scheduled debit without pausing or cancelling. The plan and the mandate are untouched and the next instalment generates normally.
  • First instalment on a still-registering mandate (D43). When a SIP is set up alongside a mandate that is not yet Active, the plan sits in Registered and its first instalment does not generate until the mandate goes Active, or the setup unwinds if the mandate is Rejected. The existing Registered state absorbs this wait, so J8 needs no new plan state for it.
  • Cut-off and NAV per instalment. Each instalment is an order subject to the same cut-off and NAV-applicability rules as a lumpsum, so a debit landing close to the cut-off may catch the next day's NAV. This is the J6 spine's concern, inherited unchanged per instalment.
  • Non-business-day scheduling. An instalment date falling on a holiday or non-settlement day shifts per the scheme and rail calendar. A value-and-timing detail on the schedule, not a change to the lifecycle.
  • Attribution on every instalment. Each child order carries its ARN and EUIN attribution, so the trail-commission and compliance trail is per instalment, not just per plan. Inherited from the Order reference build scope.
  • Persona overlays. A minor's SIP is operated by the guardian until majority, when the lifecycle freeze (owned by J2) interrupts it; an NRI SIP funds from an NRE or NRO account whose class the mandate and folio already carry. These change the funding and authority context, not the plan spine.

5.Entities and states exercised

The references and journeys J8 draws on. Per the precedence rule J8 invents no state, and unlike J5 it finds no gap: the plan states it needs are all carried by the Order reference, and the pending-mandate wait is absorbed by the existing Registered state.

Reference or journeyWhat J8 exercises
OrderThe systematic-plan entity and its lifecycle, Registered, Active, Paused, Completed, Cancelled; the plan-spawns-child-order separation and the one-plan-to-many-orders relationship; and the rule that a failed instalment is an instalment event, not a plan event.
J5 (mandate)By reference: the live mandate the plan attaches to and debits against, including its per-debit ceiling and the pre-debit notification the framework requires before each debit.

Key principle: J8 confirms the rows it exercises rather than adding new ones

J8 was the natural exerciser of three open rows, and drawing it tested whether their recommended positions hold. D9, the pause policy, stays genuinely open and is routed to Product with a proposed threshold. D43, the order-to-mandate attachment, holds: a plan whose mandate is still registering sits in Registered until the mandate is Active, with no new state needed. D44, the mandate-to-plan cardinality, holds: a single mandate backing several SIPs on one account is the path the reuse fork draws. J8 adds no register row and flags no reference gap.

Open: what hardens J8

J8 is drawn against a settled D4 and exercises D43 and D44, both of which held when a real flow leaned on them, so they need confirmation with Engineering rather than rework. The one genuinely open call is D9, the pause threshold and retry behaviour, which is a Product decision and the part of this journey that lands as a proposal rather than a settled rule. Closing D9 with Product is what moves J8 from decision-surfacing to governing. None of this blocks the money-out journeys, J9 redemption next, then the J10 and J11 plans that reuse this lifecycle shape.
