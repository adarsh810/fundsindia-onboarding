J9: Redemption

The money-out flow in depth: the redemption branch of the order spine drawn fully, gated by unencumbered units and lock-in, reduced by exit load, and tailed by a payout that a single attribute, residential status, forks into a withheld-at-source obligation for an NRI and a clean payout for a resident.

Statusv0.1: Decision-surfacing draft
DateJune 2026
AudienceEngineering & Product
TypeJourney document (not a reference, not a PRD)

What this document is

The governing view of how a redemption runs. A redemption is a sale of units back to the fund at the applicable NAV, money out, one scheme, and unlike a purchase it needs no payment mandate. J9 draws the pre-flight gates that money-out alone carries, unencumbered units, lock-in, and the exit-load haircut, the spine through to a payout measured in working days after the NAV, and the persona fork the Investor reference calls a fork in the payout rather than a footnote: an NRI or OCI redemption is withheld at source while a resident's is not, with proceeds routed by repatriability to an NRE or NRO account. It draws on Order for the redemption lifecycle, Folio for encumbrance and repatriability, and Investor for the TDS overlay.

What it is not

Not a reference and not a PRD. It is the one-scheme, one-time money-out flow. The recurring money-out, the SWP, is J11 and reuses this spine on a schedule; the two-scheme money-out, the switch and STP, is J10 and carries a buy leg driven off this sell leg. Both are out of scope here. It brushes D2, the requirement that an NRI or OCI be Validated to transact, applied as settled. One architectural boundary shapes the payout and is surfaced here: because cost-basis lot accounting is out of V1, the platform reflects the gain and the TDS the AMC and RTA compute rather than computing them itself.

1.The spine

Redemption is the simplest order to price and the richest to pay out. It is simple to price because there is no incoming money to clear first: a redemption registered before the cut-off takes the same day's NAV, with none of the funds-realisation dependency a purchase carries. It is rich to pay out because the tail, the proceeds landing in a bank account some working days later, is where the gates bite and where residential status forks the whole thing. The spine below runs the gates, then the common order states, ending when proceeds reach the registered bank account.

flowchart TD
  A([Redemption intent, units or amount, a scheme in a folio]) --> B{Pre-flight gates}
  B -->|units encumbered, lock-in not elapsed, or KYC not valid| X1([Blocked, see failure taxonomy])
  B -->|unencumbered units, lock-in elapsed, KYC valid| C([Placed])
  C --> D[RoutedToRail, handed to the rail, no funding leg to clear]
  D --> E([Acknowledged, applicable NAV determinable from the cut-off])
  E --> F([Allotted, redemption value computed at NAV, exit load applied])
  F --> G([Settled, proceeds reach the registered bank account, T plus working days])

Two things separate this from the purchase spine. At RoutedToRail there is no inbound money leg to initiate, so the order is simply handed to the rail; the money movement is the outbound payout at settlement. And between Allotted and Settled sits the persona fork: for an NRI or OCI the redemption value is reduced by capital-gains TDS withheld at source before the net reaches the account, while a resident receives the full proceeds. That fork is drawn in Section 2. The Failed and Reversed edges are the J6 edges, with redemption-specific failure modes in Section 3.

Who acts at each step

StepActing party (by role)What the firm does
Pre-flight gatesThe firm.Checks unencumbered units against the folio and RTA record, lock-in against the scheme attributes, and KYC status. The cheapest place to catch a redemption that cannot complete.
Place and routeThe firm originates; the RTA and AMC register and price.Constructs and places the redemption, then hands it to the rail and waits. No money leaves the investor; units leave the register.
Compute value and TDSThe AMC and RTA.Strike the NAV, compute the redemption value, apply the exit load, and for an NRI or OCI compute and withhold the capital-gains TDS. The firm reflects these; in V1 it does not compute the gain, because it holds no cost basis.
Pay out and settleThe AMC, through the RTA and the rail.Pays the proceeds to the registered bank account, routed by repatriability for a non-resident. The firm reconciles against the RTA feed; money never rests with the firm.

2.Decision points

One genuine fork that reroutes the payout, drawn, and one gate cluster that decides whether the redemption proceeds at all, tabled.

The residential-status fork: TDS and repatriability

This is the signature of the journey. The same redemption event produces a fundamentally different obligation depending on a single attribute, residential status, and then a second attribute, repatriability, decides where the proceeds land. The Investor reference is emphatic that this is a fork in the payout flow, not a footnote, so it is drawn.

flowchart TD
  P([Allotted, redemption value computed]) --> Q{Residential status of the holder}
  Q -->|resident| M1([Full proceeds paid out, no TDS, self-assessed via advance tax])
  Q -->|NRI or OCI| M2[Capital-gains TDS withheld at source, certificate issued]
  M2 --> R{Repatriability of the folio}
  R -->|NRE or FCNR funded, repatriable| N1([Net proceeds to an NRE account, remittable abroad])
  R -->|NRO funded, non-repatriable| N2([Net proceeds to an NRO account, limited remittance])

For a resident there is no TDS on a mutual fund redemption; the investor self-assesses through advance tax, and the proceeds land whole. For an NRI or OCI, tax is withheld at source on each redemption and a certificate is issued, and the net is routed by the folio's repatriability tag, which follows the account that funded the holding: NRE or FCNR funding is repatriable and the proceeds can be remitted abroad, NRO funding is non-repatriable subject to the limited remittance allowance. A DTAA can reduce the Indian withholding where the investor furnishes a Tax Residency Certificate and a Form 10F, which is a value change on the withholding rather than a reroute, so it is bulleted in Section 4.

The pre-flight gate cluster

Money-out is gated where money-in is not, because a redemption depends on units being available and unencumbered. The three gates differ in effect: two can block the redemption outright, one only reduces the proceeds.

GateWhat it checksEffect
Unencumbered unitsWhether the units are free, or lien-marked or pledged. Only unencumbered units can be redeemed; the encumbrance is marked on the folio by the RTA.Blocks the encumbered portion. The free balance can still redeem.
Lock-inWhether a lock-in has elapsed, for example the three-year ELSS lock-in, applied per investment tranche.Blocks the locked tranche until it matures. Other tranches are unaffected.
Exit loadWhether the holding period falls inside an exit-load band defined by the scheme.Does not block; reduces the proceeds by the load. A haircut, not a stop.

3.Failure taxonomy

Redemption breaks at two points: at the pre-flight gate, before anything is priced, and at the payout tail, after the value is fixed but before the money lands. The second kind is the dangerous one, because units have already left the register.

How it breaksCauseWhere it lands, and recovery
Insufficient unencumbered unitsThe free balance is less than the requested redemption, because units are pledged, lien-marked, or simply fewer than asked.Blocked at pre-flight, before Placed. Redeem the available free balance, or release the encumbrance first.
Lock-in not elapsedA redemption against a tranche still inside its lock-in.Blocked at pre-flight. The tranche becomes redeemable when the lock-in matures; other tranches are unaffected.
Below-minimum residualA partial redemption that would leave a balance below the scheme's minimum.Handled per scheme rule, commonly by forcing the redemption to full rather than leaving an unviable residual. Surface the choice rather than silently converting it.
Cut-off missedThe redemption is registered after the redemption cut-off.Not a failure: it takes the next applicable NAV. A timing shift the investor should see, not an error.
Payout cannot landThe registered bank account is closed, frozen, or invalid when the proceeds are paid, after Allotted.A post-allotment failure: units are gone but money cannot land. Resolves by correcting the bank mandate and re-paying; if it has to be unwound it is a Reversed, not a Failed, because allotment already happened.
Erroneous redemption backed outA redemption that progressed to or past allotment and must be undone.Lands as Reversed, a compensating transaction on the books, not a deletion. Owned by the J6 spine.

Red flag: the payout tail is where redemption is most exposed

The gates are cheap and the pricing is simple, but the tail is not. Once units are redeemed, the obligation to pay exists, so a bad bank account, a wrong repatriability route sending an NRI's proceeds to the wrong account class, or a misapplied TDS are all errors after the point of no easy return. Sending repatriable proceeds to an NRO account, or paying gross where TDS was due, are the two that carry real regulatory and remittance consequences. The tail deserves the design attention, not the pricing.

4.Special cases

Variants that change a value or add a check without rerouting the redemption spine. Listed, never drawn.

  • Full versus partial redemption. A full redemption closes the position and may empty the folio; a partial leaves a residual that must clear the scheme minimum, which is the below-minimum case in Section 3.
  • By units versus by amount. A redemption can be expressed in units or in rupees; an amount request is converted to units at the applicable NAV, so the exact units leaving are known only once the NAV is struck. A rounding and residual detail, not a lifecycle change.
  • Exit-load bands. The load depends on the holding period of the specific units being redeemed, so the order of units matters and follows the scheme's convention. The band reduces proceeds; it never blocks.
  • ELSS and other lock-ins. Applied per investment tranche, so a folio can hold redeemable and locked units at once. The gate blocks only the locked tranche.
  • DTAA relief for a non-resident. A Tax Residency Certificate plus a Form 10F can reduce the Indian withholding on an NRI redemption. It changes the withholding rate, not the flow, and depends on the investor furnishing the documents.
  • Gross-to-net representation. For an NRI the investor should see the gross redemption value, the exit load, the TDS withheld, and the net, as distinct figures. The platform displays these; it does not compute the gain in V1, so the TDS figure is the one the RTA and AMC return, not one the platform derives.
  • Repatriation certification. Remitting NRO or sale proceeds abroad uses Forms 15CA and 15CB. This is a downstream servicing step that follows a redemption, not a step inside the redemption spine.
  • Persona authority. A minor's redemption is operated by the guardian until majority; a joint folio redeems per its operation mode. These set who may place the redemption, not how it runs.
  • High-value reporting. A redemption above the prescribed threshold feeds SFT reporting. A reporting consequence on the side, not a gate.
  • SWP reuse. The recurring money-out plan, J11, emits a redemption instalment on a schedule that runs this exact spine, needing only available unencumbered units on each date and no mandate.

Key principle: in V1 the platform reflects the gain, it does not compute it

Cost-basis lot accounting is scoped out of V1, so the platform does not hold the purchase cost of the units being redeemed. The capital gain, and therefore the NRI or OCI capital-gains TDS, is computed by the AMC and RTA, who hold the unit register and the cost basis, and the platform reflects the net proceeds it receives. This is the cost-basis decision meeting the redemption payout, and it bounds what the platform can show before a redemption is confirmed, which is the open call in Section 6.

Open: what hardens J9

J9 is drawn against settled or applied decisions, D2, D6, D27, and D28, so its spine and gates are stable. The one new call it forces is D45: in V1 the platform cannot derive an NRI's capital-gains TDS itself, so the product question is what it shows before a redemption is confirmed, an exact net from an RTA pre-trade computation if one is available, a clear gross-only view with the net revealed at settlement, or an estimate the platform is honest about not being able to compute. Closing D45 with Product and the architect, against the cost-basis-out-of-V1 boundary, is what moves J9 from decision-surfacing to governing. None of this blocks Wave 3, where J10 switch and STP carry this sell leg with a buy leg driven off it, and J11 SWP repeats this spine on a schedule.
