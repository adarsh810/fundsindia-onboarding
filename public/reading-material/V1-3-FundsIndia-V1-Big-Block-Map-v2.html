How to read this, and what it supersedes

This map reflects the live working decisions as of this session. It deliberately departs from the now-stale Build Strategy and Blueprint on three points, reconciled in a single later pass: portfolio review is dissection plus a separate allocation-guidance block rather than an "Ideal Portfolio recommendation"; Fixed Deposits move to V1.5/V2; Account Aggregator is in V1. Bands are stacked experience-to-substrate; the order is architectural, not a build sequence. v2 adds the persona-scope marker, a Servicing block, the cost-basis deferral, and per-layer commentary.

V1 persona scope, the governing boundary

V1 serves natural personas only: Resident Individual, NRI, OCI, and Minor (guardian-operated), with Joint as a holding-mode overlay rather than a persona. Foreign National resident is recommended out of the initial set, pending confirmation. HUF and the non-individual entities are deferred to the Private Wealth phase. NRI is not just an onboarding branch: it threads TDS, FATCA, and repatriability through Transactions, Catalog, and the Ledger.

Five constraints that shape every block below

  • Goal is the organising lens. Holdings are a view under goals, not a peer container.
  • Signals are capture and internal-read only in V1. No investor-facing read, no action or write-back.
  • Dissection surfaces state, never judge. "Stock is 8% of equity", not "too much weightage".
  • Allocation guidance is MFD-incidental. The MFD-RIA boundary is under live SEBI review, so it is built for the conservative case and passes a strict Compliance gate.
  • The experience layer is throughput-bound to two UI devs through all of V1.

Layer 1
Investor Experience
The surfaces the investor touches.
Commentary The thinnest layer by headcount and the most constrained. Three surfaces, one web codebase and one Flutter codebase covering both mobile platforms, each fronted by its own BFF so the experience can evolve independently of the services beneath it. The two-UI-dev ceiling is the single hardest constraint on V1 sequencing: experience work cannot be parallelised the way the nine-strong backend can, so the plan bends around it rather than wishing it away. For competitive research the sharper question is not which screens a rival has, but how much of the goal lens actually reaches the surface, because most platforms bolt goals on as a tab rather than making them the home.

Layer 2
Goal-Led Product
The V1 investor proposition, organised around the goal.
Commentary The differentiating layer, and the one carrying the load-bearing bet. Goal is not a feature here; it is the organising entity, with a full lifecycle of creation, articulation, modification, and a fulfillment moment, and holdings rendered as a view beneath it. The allocation-guidance block is the bridge that makes the goal actionable for a mass-affluent user who needs help getting from intent to instrument, and it is also the most compliance-loaded surface in V1, which is why it sits inside the MFD-incidental envelope and clears the Compliance gate. The thing to watch competitively is goal-adoption depth: not whether a rival has goals, but whether users personalise and fund them, because that is the variable the whole thesis rests on.

Layer 3
Catalog, Transact, Portfolio & Servicing
Finding an instrument, moving money, seeing what you hold, and keeping the account current.
Commentary The transactional spine of the investor's day, plus the long tail that earns trust. The catalog is the strongly-typed home that carries type, classification and tax tagging from day one. Discovery is deliberately split: objective browse, filter and sort is safe, while the governed ranking console is where business intervention is allowed but supervised. Transactions own the Order entity and route through the rails. Portfolio is dissection only, stating facts and never prescribing, with benchmark comparison disclaimed and category-appropriate. Servicing and account-lifecycle flows live here too, and they carry several of the decisions still open with Legal and Compliance. Competitive research should look hard at how rivals handle the servicing long tail, since most under-invest there and it is precisely where trust is won or lost.

Layer 4
Canonical Substrate
The invisible core everything else depends on.
Commentary The unglamorous core that earns its place in retrospect. One canonical entity model, an immutable event spine that everything downstream derives from, and a double-entry bitemporal ledger that is a reconciliation projection of RTA truth rather than a replacement for it. The relationship substrate sits here because the commercial-relationship and nominee-and-family graphs are V1 even though the RM-facing surface is not; in V1 they ingest to the legacy CRM. This layer is invisible to the customer and is exactly what most competitors did not build, which is why they cannot answer arbitrary questions about past state or reconcile cleanly. It is the moat that never shows up in a feature comparison.

Layer 5
Intelligence Seam
Thin in V1: the seam the future engine plugs into, built so it never needs a retrofit.
Commentary Thin on purpose. Two planes: a behavioural clickstream plane kept separate from the domain event spine, and the signal-capture substrate built against a constraint-spec so V1 modelling decisions are testable against what the future engine will need. The discipline is the four-flow boundary: capture and internal read are on, investor-facing read and any actioning are off. This is the seam, not the engine. The only reason it is in V1 is that the entity and event shape the engine will reason over is expensive to retrofit, so the cheap thing is to get the shape right and capture the signals now, and the expensive thing is deferred. Capture carries a consent and purpose-limitation shadow even when nothing surfaces, which is why it leans on the consent ledger below.

Layer 6
Integration & Rails
Every third party behind the anti-corruption layer. Rail-agnostic, vendor-neutral.
Commentary Every third party behind one anti-corruption layer, one adapter per domain, so the core never takes a vendor's shape even when a single vendor spans two domains. FP/Cybrilla with Razorpay carries the MF and payment path, HyperVerge the identity path, and an AA rail the financial picture, all left vendor-neutral in the governing docs because the market moves and naming implies endorsement. A platform constraint shapes this whole layer: under the MFD posture the platform never pools client money, so money moves through the rails while the platform reconciles rather than holds. Competitively the rail choices are table stakes; the real differentiator is the ACL discipline that keeps the core swappable when a rail changes.

Layer 7
Cross-Cutting Platform
Spans every layer above. Built from day one, not bolted on.
Commentary Spans every layer and is built from day one rather than bolted on. The compliance and identity infrastructure is the part no vendor provides and the part most easily underestimated: authentication and access control, the consent ledger, PII tokenisation, and DPDP rights tooling, all of which the signal-capture work depends on for its lawful basis. MarTech is instrumentation and hooks only; running campaigns is a growth function, not a platform block, and is covered by the experienced dev and PM. The reason this layer is cross-cutting rather than a phase is that retrofitting consent and access control after the fact is how platforms acquire their most expensive debt.

Held with explicit triggers, not forgotten. The substrate above is built so these slot in without rework.
Commentary Naming the deferred set precisely is what lets the team say no with confidence now. The substrate above is shaped so each of these slots in without a rewrite: the AMC modelled as an entity so an in-house AMC is clean later, the event spine carrying what the intelligence engine will need, the rails behind an ACL so a new rail is an adapter not a rebuild.
